# ABSTRACT 

Image deblurring is a fundamental task in computer vision, yet existing methods mainly specialize in isolated scenarios. Consequently, the deblurring performance of these models drops significantly across scenarios with different cameras and image contents. This motivates the development of a unified motion deblurring framework that can handle diverse blur patterns. To this end, we construct a multi-source dataset and develop a data pruning and quality control pipeline. Then, to restore blur images with various motion blur kernels across different scenarios, we propose a unified motion deblurring framework via Fourier Convolution Learning. Existing deblurring networks usually rely on pixel-level mappings and overlook the convolutional nature of blur. Thus, the network mostly fits the image content itself rather than learning the convolution process, making it difficult to handle multi-source blur images. Instead of relying on spatial mapping, we perform blur-adaptive global filter learning in Fourier space, which avoids the limited spatial support of kernel estimation. Exten-sive experiments demonstrate that our unified model effectively handles motion blurs across diverse sources and scenes, achieving competitive performance across both in-domain and OOD bench-marks. # 1 INTRODUCTION 

Image deblurring, as a fundamental subtask of image restoration, aims to recover latent sharp images from blur in-puts Nah et al. (2017); Kupyn et al. (2019). In real-world scenarios, the formation of a blurry image B is formulated as the spatial convolution of a sharp latent image S with a motion blur kernel kblur , along with additive noise n Fergus et al. (2006); Pan et al. (2016): 

B = S ⊛ kblur + n , (1) where ⊛ denotes the 2D convolution operator. This degradation process destroys high-frequency edge details and delicate textures, leading to visually unpleasant artifacts and severely impairing downstream vision tasks. Recently, deep learning-based approaches have achieved remarkable improvements in image deblurring Zamir et al. (2021); Chen et al. (2022); Wang et al. (2022). Existing methods predominantly develop specialized frameworks on isolated benchmark datasets. However, as shown in Table 1, real-world motion blurs exhibit substantial divergence across datasets due to disparate cameras (GoPro, Sony, CHRONOS), distinct resolutions, and diverse scenes (outdoor streetscapes, pedestrians, night-time, low-light). Consequently, models trained on a single isolated scenario suffer from severe performance drops when deployed across unseen environments. To alleviate cross-domain degradation, recent All-in-One restoration models attempt to handle diverse corruptions within a unified framework Li et al. (2022); Potlapalli et al. (2023). Nonetheless, their performance on motion deblurring remains constrained. To this end, developing a unified motion deblurring framework becomes highly imperative. As shown in Fig. 1, we present a unified motion deblurring framework, termed UniDeblur , driven by Fourier Convo-lution Learning (FCL). On the data side, recognizing that high-quality datasets are essential for reliable training and evaluation, we design a multi-source training/evaluation protocol equipped with a dedicated data pruning and quality control pipeline. This pipeline removes samples with obvious quality issues, such as spatial misalignment and residual blur in the ground truth, to prevent the network from fitting erroneous degradation patterns. Consequently, it ensures clean supervision for model optimization and reduces the influence of obvious acquisition and annotation artifacts across both in-domain and out-of-domain (OOD) benchmarks. On the framework side, mainstream networks are typically confined to spatial pixel-to-pixel black-box mapping, often overlooking the convolutional nature of motion blur. To overcome this limitation, we elevate the restoration paradigm to blur-adaptive convolution learning. By exploiting the Convolution Theorem, FCL reformulates spatial kernel esti-mation as element-wise multiplication in the Fourier space. As shown in Fig. 2, this formulation enables the network to predict blur-adaptive frequency filters and dynamically modulate the deep features. Thus, it captures degradation-aware kernels in diverse motion blur scenes with minimal computational overhead. The main contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose UniDeblur, a unified motion deblurring framework with a multi-source training and evaluation protocol, ensuring effective training supervision and evaluation. • We design the Fourier Convolution Learning module, which reformulates complex spatial kernel prediction into efficient frequency-domain multiplication, enabling blur-adaptive filter learning to effectively capture diverse input-specific blurs. 2Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2027 • Extensive experiments on both in-domain and OOD benchmarks demonstrate that our method achieves com-petitive restoration performance and superior cross-domain generalization ability. 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

Deep Image Deblurring In recent years, deep learning-based methods have achieved significant success in image deblurring Nah et al. (2017); Tao et al. (2018); Zamir et al. (2021). Pioneering approaches attempted to estimate the blur kernel using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and then restore the latent image via non-blind deconvolu-tion Sun et al. (2015). However, these methods struggle with spatially-variant blurs in dynamic scenes and are sensitive to noise. Consequently, almost all recent methods have shifted towards end-to-end training paradigms, mapping blurry images directly to their sharp counterparts. To achieve better performance, various architectural improvements have been proposed, ranging from multi-stage CNNs (MPRNet Zamir et al. (2021), DMPHN Zhang et al. (2019)) to ad-vanced Vision Transformers (Uformer Wang et al. (2022), Restormer Zamir et al. (2022)). Despite their success, these methods primarily focus on pixel-level restoration, merely learning the residual between blur and sharp image pairs while overlooking the convolutional nature of the blur process. To revisit kernel learning, some recent works introduce kernel prior-assisted models: UFPNet Fang et al. (2023) pre-trains a flow-based model to estimate spatially variant kernel maps, and PGDN Fang et al. (2025) introduces a parameterized motion kernel estimator. However, kernels learned in the spatial domain are often limited in size and capacity for convolution. 

Frequency Domain Methods for Vision Tasks A growing literature corpus has demonstrated the effectiveness of extracting information from the frequency domain for various vision tasks Rippel et al. (2015); Qin et al. (2020); Rao et al. (2021). Since the Convolution Theorem states that convolution in the spatial domain is equivalent to element-wise multiplication in the frequency domain, Fourier space presents a tractable framework for handling large-scale spatial transformations. For example, LaMa Suvorov et al. (2022) applies Fast Fourier Convolution (FFC) to capture a wider receptive field for image inpainting. GFNet Rao et al. (2021) learns long-term spatial dependencies in the frequency domain for image classification. For image deblurring, DeepRFT Mao et al. (2023) introduces an additive paradigm of Res-FFT-ReLU to recognize blur patterns. LoFormer Mao et al. (2024) and FFTformer Kong et al. (2023) further explore frequency information for self-attention. SegDeblur Kim et al. (2024) and Self-Deblur Kim et al. (2025) try to predict the image-level deconvolution kernel in logarithmic Fourier space for reliable prior information. However, these approaches merely utilize frequency representations for additive feature aggregation or auxiliary prior, leaving the potential of convolution learning in the feature space largely underexplored for the deblurring task. 

Unified Image Restoration Nowadays, almost all existing deblurring models are trained and evaluated on specific datasets (GoPro Nah et al. (2017) for global camera shake, LOLBlur Zhou et al. (2022) for night-time blur). This reliance on single-dataset training severely limits their generalization capability when deployed in complex, real-world open scenarios. Recently, All-in-One image restoration models Li et al. (2022); Potlapalli et al. (2023); Hu et al. (2025); Tang et al. (2026) attempted to solve multiple degradations (such as noise, rain, and blur) using a single unified network. However, denoising and deraining are additive degradations requiring local spatial aggregation, whereas motion blur is a convolutional degradation that demands large-scale deformation and phase shift correction, so that their performance on deblurring remains constrained. DeMoE Feijoo et al. (2026) proposed a Mixture-of-Experts decoder for unified image deblurring, aiming to address both motion and defocus blur within a single model. However, compared to the diverse range of available motion blur benchmarks, existing defocus datasets Abuolaim & Brown (2020); Lee et al. (2021); Ruan et al. (2021); Li et al. (2023b) suffer from several inherent issues, such as color shifts, pixel misalignment, and limited scene diversity. Therefore, instead of mixing multiple degradations, we specifically target a Unified Motion Deblurring framework which learns the essence of blur degradation via Fourier Convolution Learning.

From Spatial Convolution to Frequency Filtering. As formulated in Eq. 1, motion blur is fundamentally a convo-lution process. However, most existing deep networks rely on a direct pixel-to-pixel mapping strategy B → ˆS in the spatial domain. This black-box mapping merely attempts to fit spatial differences while overlooking the convolutional nature of the blur degradation. Forcing standard CNNs to implicitly learn complex, large-scale inverse convolutions inevitably leads to severe receptive field bottlenecks and optimization difficulties. To this end, we shift the convolution learning process to the Fourier space. According to the Convolution Theorem, spatial convolution is mathematically equivalent to element-wise multiplication in the frequency domain: 

F(B) = F(S) ⊙ F (k blur ) → S = F−1 (F(B) ⊙ F (k deblur )) , (3) where kdeblur is the inverse kernel of kblur , F denotes the 2D Real Fast Fourier Transform (RFFT2D), F−1 is the Inverse RFFT2D, and ⊙ is the Hadamard product. This conversion simply reformulates the intractable spatial kernel estimation into a simpler multiplication. 

Fourier Convolution Learning (FCL). Existing frequency-based architectures fail to fully exploit this property. For instance, DeepRFT Mao et al. (2023) adopts an additive paradigm: 

xout = F−1 (F(x in ) + Hθ (F(x in ))) , (4) which treats frequency representations merely as additive residual features rather than convolution kernels. Conversely, while GFNet Rao et al. (2021) utilizes frequency-domain multiplication: 

xout = F−1 (F(x in ) ⊙ W) , (5) it learns a static global filter W that acts merely as a degradation-agnostic prompt, lacking the adaptability to handle varying, image-specific motion blurs. Inspired by Eq. 3, we propose the FCL module (Fig. 3(b)) to instantiate a blur-adaptive convolution process: 

xout = F−1 (F(x in ) ⊙ H θk (F(x in ))) . (6) Specifically, FCL transforms xin from spatial domain to frequency domain, where a lightweight non-linear network 

Hθk dynamically predicts blur-adaptive global filters F(k) . These blur-adaptive filters are then multiplied with F(x in )

before applying the IRFFT2D. This design enables the model to perform blur-adaptive convolution in Fourier space.

To rigorously train and evaluate our unified model, we establish a comprehensive multi-source dataset, categorized into in-domain training sets and out-of-distribution (OOD) test sets. 

In-Domain Training Datasets. We construct our training corpus using six high-quality datasets: GoPro Nah et al. (2017), HIDE Shen et al. (2019), REDS Nah et al. (2021), RSBlur Rim et al. (2022), RealBlur Rim et al. (2020), and LOLBlur Zhou et al. (2022). These datasets feature pristine ground truths (GTs) and cover diverse scenarios (synthetic/real blurs, low-light, and dynamic motions), providing a robust foundation for unified deblurring. 

OOD Testing and Quality Control. To evaluate cross-domain generalization, we select six challenging OOD test sets: BSD1/BSD2/BSD3 Zhong et al. (2023), MIORe/VAR-MIORe Ciubotariu et al. (2025), and FoundIR Li et al. (2025). As shown in Fig. 4, these datasets inherently suffer from acquisition flaws, such as mixed degradations in MIORe, residual GT blur in BSD, and pixel misalignments in FoundIR. To ensure reliable and efficient evaluation, we systematically prune the data (Table 2). Specifically, we subsample the test sets to a manageable scale and strictly filter out flawed GT-blur pairs. Additionally, high-resolution training images are cropped into patches for better I/O. 

Evaluation Protocol. To mitigate evaluation bias from inconsistent GT quality, we adopt a hybrid protocol. For in-domain datasets, we use PSNR and SSIM. 4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

UniDeblur Architecture. We introduce two variants, UniDeblur-B and UniDeblur-L. As shown in Fig. 3(a), UniDe-blur adopts a 3-scale U-Net architecture, with detailed configurations summarized in Table 5. 

Comparative Methods. We selected representative end-to-end methods MIMO-UNet Cho et al. (2021), Deep-RFT Mao et al. (2023), NAFNet Chen et al. (2022), LoFormer Mao et al. (2024), EVSSM Kong et al. (2025), PromptIR Potlapalli et al. (2023), MoCEIR Zamfir et al. (2025) and BioIR Cui et al. (2026) from both motion de-blurring and All-in-One image restoration. We retrain all baselines under the unified training protocol. In addition, we calculate the computational cost (FLOPs) and the total training memory (Mem) of each model for comparison. 

Configuration. All models are trained for 300K iterations using Adam Kingma & Ba (2014) ( β1 = 0 .9, β2 = 0 .999 ,weight decay 1 ×10 −2), initial learning rate 5 ×10 −4. The learning rate is steadily decreased to 1 ×10 −6 with the cosine annealing schedule. The batch size is set to 16, and the patch size is 256 × 256 .

Loss Function. All of the models are supervised exclusively by deblurring loss, defined as the sum of the L1 loss ( ℓ1)and the Frequency Reconstruction loss ( ℓf req ): ℓtotal = ℓ1 + 0 .01 ℓf req .4.2 COMPARISONS WITH REPRESENTATIVE METHODS

While methods like DeepRFT demonstrate the effectiveness of frequency-domain learning over the spatial baseline MIMO-UNet, they typically impose severe training memory burdens due to dense spatial-frequency transformations. # 5 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we proposed UniDeblur, a unified framework for motion deblurring based on Fourier Convolution Learning (FCL). FCL performs blur-adaptive filtering in the Fourier space, allowing the network to explicitly model the convolutional nature of motion blur. We also constructed a multi-source training and evaluation protocol with data quality control. Experiments on in-domain and OOD datasets show that FCL improves deblurring performance and generalization across different blur sources, with moderate computational overhead.
