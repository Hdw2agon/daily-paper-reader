## ABSTRACT 

Pretrained diffusion Transformers provide powerful generative priors for image restoration, yet adapting them to structure-sensitive tasks remains challenging. Existing methods mainly rely on dense low-resolution features as conditions, which often entangle salient objects with dominant backgrounds and provide limited abstraction for recurring structures across scales and samples. We propose MOSAIC, an object-centric representation learning framework that learns compact structural conditions for generative model adaptation. MOSAIC organizes degraded observations into structural slots that capture complementary object-level and layout-level evidence, integrates them across resolutions, and retrieves recurring priors from a shared prototype memory to complement incomplete image-specific information. The resulting representations are injected into selected layers of a frozen diffusion Transformer through lightweight residual modulation, preserving its pretrained generative prior. We instantiate MOSAIC for remote sensing image super-resolution, where small objects, repetitive structures, and large-scale variation provide a challenging testbed. Experiments on UCMerced, AID, and RSSCN7 show consistent PSNR improvements across ×2 and ×4 settings and favorable LPIPS results at ×4. Qualitative results further demonstrate improved preservation of small objects, structural boundaries, and repetitive spatial patterns.

## 1 Introduction 

High-resolution remote sensing imagery is essential for fine-grained Earth observation and supports applications such as urban monitoring, environmental analysis, and disaster assessment. Recent surveys and real-world SR studies highlight both the rapid progress of deep-learning-based super-resolution and the remaining challenges under realistic degradations (Moser et al., 2023; Sun & Chen, 2024). However, sensor limitations, complex imaging conditions, and transmission constraints often obscure small objects, structural boundaries, and repetitive textures; recent generative SR methods likewise emphasize the difficulty of maintaining fidelity under real degradations (Han et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026). Remote sensing image super-resolution (RSISR) aims to recover high-resolution observations while preserving both large-scale layouts and fine local structures. Compared with natural images, remote sensing scenes exhibit substantial scale variation, dense spatial patterns, and small foreground objects embedded in large background regions. Therefore, effective RSISR requires not only plausible texture generation but also faithful reconstruction of object geometry and regular spatial arrangements. Existing RSISR methods have evolved from convolutional and Transformer architectures to diffusion-based generative models, with recent DiT-based approaches benefiting from strong generative priors and long-range modeling (Chen et al., 2021b; Xiao et al., 2024; Huo et al., 2026). However, they typically inject LR observations as dense spatial conditions (Kim et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2026). As illustrated in Figure 1(a), such dense conditions entangle foreground objects with dominant backgrounds, weakening small targets and structural boundaries during denoising. This reveals that effective RSISR depends not only on the generative capacity of the backbone, but also on how conditional evidence is organized. Figure 1(b) highlights three characteristics of remote sensing scenes that motivate our design. (i) Object-centric structural organization. Foreground objects and structural regions occupy limited spatial areas but carry critical geometric information, which is easily diluted in dense representations dominated by backgrounds. (ii) Cross-scale complementarity. Coarser representations capture stable layouts and long-range structures, whereas finer ones preserve boundaries, textures, and small objects, making multi-scale cues inherently complementary. (iii) Incomplete image-specific evidence. Severe degradation may erase image-specific structural cues, while similar object layouts and textures frequently recur across remote sensing scenes, providing reusable structural knowledge. Motivated by these observations, we propose a Multi-Scale Object-Centric Slot Adaptation framework with Image-Guided Prototype Conditioning, termed MOSAIC , for RSISR. As illustrated in Figure 1(c), MOSAIC equips a frozen DiT4SR backbone with object-centric conditioning. It organizes dense LR features into compact structural slots, exploits cross-scale complementarity, and retrieves shared prototypes to supplement degraded image-specific evidence. The resulting conditions guide foreground-aware reconstruction while preserving the pretrained generative prior. As shown in Figure 1(d), MOSAIC recovers more coherent object structures, sharper boundaries, and finer textures. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce an object-centric representation learning paradigm for diffusion Transformer adaptation, trans-forming dense LR features into compact structural slots while preserving the pretrained generative prior. • We develop a multi-scale structural representation learning mechanism that fuses complementary slot represen-tations across resolutions to capture both global layouts and fine-grained local structures. • We incorporate image-guided retrieval from a shared prototype memory as residual context for fused slots, providing a complementary conditioning path alongside image-specific features. 

## 2 Related Work 

2.1 Remote Sensing Image Super-Resolution 

Remote sensing image super-resolution reconstructs high-resolution images from low-resolution observations while preserving scene layouts and structures such as roads, buildings, fields, and small objects. Large-scale variation, repeated textures, and complex backgrounds make this challenging. CNN methods recover local details: SRCNN, EDSR, and RCAN use end-to-end convolution, deep residual learning, and channel attention (Dong et al., 2014; Lim et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2018b), while RASAF combines residual aggregation and split attention to fuse multi-level remote-sensing features (Chen et al., 2021b). These methods are effective for texture recovery but limited in long-range structural modeling; early CNN designs therefore struggle to maintain globally consistent layouts over large scenes. Transformer approaches extend the receptive field: IPT transfers knowledge across restoration tasks, SwinIR and HAT use window and cross-window attention (Chen et al., 2021a; Liang et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2023), and TTST selects informative tokens to suppress irrelevant regions and model long-range ground-object relations (Xiao et al., 2024). 2MOSAIC

Although attention expands context, dense representations still mix foreground structures with redundant background content. Most approaches therefore lack compact object-level conditions, making structural evidence selection difficult in complex backgrounds. 

2.2 Diffusion and DiT-based Restoration 

Diffusion models have progressed from multi-step denoising to efficient sampling and stronger architectures. DDPM formulates progressive noising and reverse denoising (Ho et al., 2020), DDIM accelerates sampling while preserving quality (Song et al., 2021), Latent Diffusion Models shift diffusion to a compressed latent space (Rombach et al., 2022), and DiT processes latent tokens with Transformers for long-range spatial modeling (Peebles & Xie, 2023). SinSR and TSD-SR distill multi-step diffusion into one-step super-resolution (Wang et al., 2024; Dong et al., 2025); these advances improve efficiency and fidelity but do not specify how structural conditions should be organized for restoration. Iterative generative priors and global latent-space modeling support structural recovery and detail synthesis. SR3 demonstrated progressive high-frequency recovery (Saharia et al., 2023), and DiT4SR injects low-resolution control latents into DiT for real-world super-resolution (Duan et al., 2025). However, dense single-scale conditions retain excessive background information and can weaken small objects, regular textures, and local structures; MOSAIC instead supplies compact multi-scale object-level conditions to a frozen DiT backbone. 

2.3 Object-Centric Representation and Prototype Priors 

Object-centric learning compresses many input tokens into a small set of slots. MONet and IODINE learn scene decom-position and multi-object representations, while Slot Attention iteratively aggregates input features into exchangeable slots (Burgess et al., 2019; Greff et al., 2019; Locatello et al., 2020). Later work improves slot stability and multi-scale modeling through Smoothing Slot Attention and multi-scale object representation (Zhao et al., 2026, 2025). However, image-specific slots do not explicitly summarize structures shared across samples. Prototype and codebook methods provide shared learned entries: VQ-VAE discretizes continuous features, VQ-VAE-2 expands capacity hierarchically, and VQGAN couples codebooks with adversarial synthesis (van den Oord et al., 2017; Razavi et al., 2019; Esser et al., 2021). Direct quantization or feature replacement can discard image-specific details. ## 3 Method 

3.1 Overall Framework 

Figure 2 summarizes MOSAIC. The LR observation ILR is resized to the VAE operating resolution and encoded into a control latent zc, while flow matching starts from noisy latent zt. The shared OCL Slot Extractor forms compact slots at the original and resized scales; MSF fuses structural layouts with fine details. We first pretrain the OCL extractor and MSF, construct a frozen prototype memory from training slots, and then optimize the prototype cross-attention and SlotFiLM modules. SlotFiLM pools the enhanced slots and predicts layer-specific modulation for selected frozen DiT4SR blocks. At each sampling step, the conditioned DiT predicts the flow-matching target, the scheduler updates zt,and the VAE Decoder produces ISR .

3.4 Image-Guided Prototype Conditioning 

The fused slots Smsf describe current-image structures, but severely degraded LR inputs can miss evidence for some objects and textures. Remote sensing scenes also contain recurring patterns. As shown in Figure 2, Image-Guided Prototype Conditioning constructs memory from multi-scale fused training slots and retrieves residual context for the current slots through cross-attention. 

Step 1: Constructing the prototype memory. We flatten MSF slots from all training images and apply KMeans; the 

M centroids form the prototype memory: 

P ∈ RM ×256 . (14) The centroids summarize recurring structures and spatial patterns in the training distribution as shared residual priors. 

Step 2: Retrieving shared structural context. The memory P remains frozen. For the current image, Smsf provides queries and P keys and values: 

Hp = PMC( Q = Smsf , K = P, V = P ), (15) 

Spmc = Smsf + αH p, (16) where PMC retrieves context relevant to the current slots. 

Step 3: Residual conditioning. With α = 0 .10 , the normalized memory output is added residually to Smsf , comple-menting image-specific evidence without replacing it. Only the PMC projections and SlotFiLM Adapter are updated; 

Spmc then conditions frozen DiT blocks through SlotFiLM. The memory is constructed offline from training slots, remains frozen thereafter, and is used at both adaptation and inference. 

## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup Datasets. We evaluate MOSAIC on UCMerced (Yang & Newsam, 2010), AID (Xia et al., 2017), and RSSCN7 (Zou et al., 2015) at ×2 and ×4, reporting PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018a), MANIQA (Yang et al., 2022), and CLIP-IQA (Wang et al., 2023). The no-reference MANIQA and CLIP-IQA metrics are evaluated only at ×4.

Implementation Details. Data are partitioned before LR generation, so held-out images are excluded from both optimization and the construction of the frozen prototype memory. Model selection and ablations use validation sets; main comparisons use held-out test sets. Detailed data splits, LR generation, and evaluation protocols are provided in the Technical Supplement . Implemented in PyTorch on 4 Tesla V100-SXM2-32GB GPUs, MOSAIC uses AdamW, FP16 mixed precision, a batch size of 1/GPU, and learning rates of 5 × 10 −7–5 × 10 −6. Unless noted, we use 8 256-D slots (3 iterations), MSF scales [1 .0, 0.75] , 16 prototypes, 4-head prototype cross-attention (64-D heads), residual scale 0.1, SlotFiLM blocks [2 , 8, 12 , 16] , and 10 sampling steps. 

4.2 Main Results Quantitative results. Table 1 reports the full-reference results (PSNR and SSIM). MOSAIC achieves the best PSNR on all three datasets under both scales. Under the ×4 setting, it improves PSNR over DiT4SR by 2.03 dB on UCMerced, 4.54 dB on AID, and 1.49 dB on RSSCN7; the corresponding ×2 gains are 2.90 dB, 5.40 dB, and 2.25 dB. These comparisons establish the overall PSNR gains; the ablations below examine the contributions of the individual components. Table 2 reports the perceptual-quality results (LPIPS, MANIQA, and CLIP-IQA). MOSAIC obtains the lowest LPIPS on both RSSCN7 and AID. The MANIQA and CLIP-IQA results vary across datasets while remaining competitive. Slot-number ablation. Figure 6 evaluates the number of object-centric slots. PSNR varies non-monotonically across the tested values and reaches its maximum of 24.0658 dB at K = 8 ; it decreases at K = 10 . This result motivates our choice of K = 8 for the final model.

This configuration, using blocks [2 , 8, 12 , 16] , achieves the best PSNR of 24.2439 dB. Early blocks receive the structural condition before the denoising features become highly specialized. Middle blocks further refine the recovered content. This placement provides a better balance between structural control and texture reconstruction than using only a limited part of the denoising network.

## 5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we presented MOSAIC, an object-centric adaptation framework for DiT-based remote sensing image super-resolution. MOSAIC organizes dense LR conditions into compact structural slots, combines cues across two scales, and retrieves shared prototypes to guide a frozen DiT backbone while preserving its generative prior. Experiments on three remote sensing datasets under ×2 and ×4 settings show consistent PSNR gains and favorable LPIPS results on the evaluated ×4 datasets. The qualitative examples illustrate more coherent local structures and finer details in the shown scenes. 

Broader Implications and Limitations. These results highlight object-centric conditioning as a promising way to adapt pretrained generative models to structure-sensitive remote sensing restoration. One limitation is that the prototype memory is constructed offline from the training distribution, which may limit adaptation to unseen sensors and degradations; developing dynamic and more transferable structural priors is left for future work.
