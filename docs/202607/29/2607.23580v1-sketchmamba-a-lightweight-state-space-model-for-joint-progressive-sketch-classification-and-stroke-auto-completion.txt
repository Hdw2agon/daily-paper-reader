Abstract. Existing vector-sketch models treat recognition and genera-tion as separate tasks, leaving a gap for streaming interfaces that must understand a drawing as it is being made. We present SketchMamba, a single causal sequence model that continuously classifies a sketch from any partial prefix while simultaneously generating its continuation. We achieve this by applying a dense per-step classification loss to a selec-tive state-space backbone. Evaluated on a 58-class subset of the Quick, Draw! dataset, SketchMamba yields 94.93% final-step accuracy and a progressive-accuracy Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.706, crossing 90% of its final accuracy by the time 70% of the strokes are drawn. In a matched-budget comparison, the 1.55 million-parameter backbone ties a causal Transformer while outperforming recurrent and convolutional baselines. Ablations confirm that the dense supervision regime, rather than the architecture alone, drives the early-prediction capability. The results demonstrate that a single causal hidden state can unify progres-sive recognition and autoregressive generation without auxiliary encoders or task-specific branching. 

Keywords: Vector Sketch · State Space Models · Progressive Recogni-tion · Sequence Modelling 

1 Introduction 

Digital ink interfaces on mobile devices make recording stroke sequences routine. Large-scale datasets like Quick, Draw! [26] permit training robust sequence mod-els for these data. Treating a drawing as an autoregressive sequence of pen dis-placements was established for online handwriting by Graves [19] and adapted for sketches by Sketch-RNN [23], using a recurrent decoder with a Gaussian-mixture output head to synthesise continuations. As streaming sketch interfaces become standard, models must process and describe these drawings as they are actively being drawn. Existing work treats recognition and generation separately. 2 Literature Review 

We organise the related work along the four axes that the present paper touches: vector-sketch understanding and generation, mainstream sequence backbones, the recent state-space-model lineage, and the methodology context that frames our matched-budget evaluation. Within each axis we synthesise rather than enu-merate; Table 1 summarises how the most directly comparable prior systems differ from SketchMamba on the dimensions that matter for streaming sketch interfaces. 

2.1 Vector sketch understanding and generation 

Two largely disjoint threads have shaped vector-sketch modelling. The recogni-tion thread treats the sketch as a static object. Early work rasterised strokes for CNNs [54]; subsequent systems combined stroke-level RNNs with CNNs [29], exploited graph structure [53], or scaled via deep hashing [51] and fine-grained retrieval [41]. Transformer encoders enabled Sketchformer [39] and the bidirec-tional, self-supervised Sketch-BERT [30]. These produce a single classification score on the finished sketch, leaving the per-prefix recognition curve undefined. The generation thread treats the sketch as a stochastic sequence. Sketch-RNN [23] models stroke offsets with a bivariate Gaussian-mixture head [7], build-ing on Graves’s LSTM handwriting decoder [19]. Subsequent generators explore 4 K. Jhaveri et al. 

CNN-RNN encoders [11], Bézier embeddings [17], compositional strokes [1], hier-archical Transformers for SVGs [9], lattice grids [37], neural-ODEs [16], abstract priors [52], creative-sketch GANs [18,5], graph-to-sequence healing [45,38], diffu-sion samplers [49], and CLIP-guided rasterisers [48]. Class identity is treated as a one-hot conditioning input rather than a prediction target. A few works sit closer to our streaming objective. Pixelor [6] trains an RL agent to race a human to recognisability but does not formalise dense per-step supervision; Sketch-R2CNN [29] attends over points before rasterising, breaking causal sequence generation. The gap remains a single causal backbone supervised at every step for both tasks. 

2.2 Sequence backbones 

Four backbone families dominate causal sequence modelling. Recurrent networks (LSTM [24], GRU [12]) have O(L) inference cost but suffer optimisation difficul-ties on long horizons. Causal dilated 1-D convolutions (WaveNet [34], TCN [2]) replace recurrence with fixed receptive fields. Transformers [47] use global self-attention but scale quadratically, motivating segment-recurrent [13], sparse [4], and linear [28] variants. Our matched-budget comparison is justified by bench-marking work showing that training recipes and scaling rules, rather than ar-chitecture, often dominate cross-paper deltas [3,31], with optimisers as a con-found [42,8]. 

2.3 State-space models for sequences 

Structured state-space models reframe long-sequence modelling as a learnable linear recurrence computed by a parallel scan. Originating with HiPPO [21] and S4 [22], subsequent variants simplify the parameterisation [43], close the lan-guage gap [14], or replace attention with long convolutions [36]. Mamba [20] in-troduces input-dependent selectivity with a hardware-aware scan; Mamba-2 [15] formalises a duality with attention. The primitive is conceptually close to other linear-attention recurrences [35,46] and provides exactly what we need: a causal hidden state at every step. 

2.4 Training methodology and evaluation context 

Our joint objective is an instance of multi-task hard parameter sharing [10,40]. The optimisation recipe (AdamW, cosine warmup, RMSNorm, EMA, bf16) fol-lows standard practice [33,32,55,25] and numerical stability findings [27]. The progressive-accuracy curve operationalises early classification for time series [50], and macro-F1 is used for class imbalance [44]. 

3 Methodology 

SketchMamba is a single causal sequence model that jointly performs sketch recognition and autoregressive sketch generation. It reads drawing actions one SketchMamba 5

at a time and maintains a single hidden state, from which it continuously pre-dicts the overall class of the sketch while simultaneously predicting the spatial coordinates and pen state of the next stroke. 

3.1 Data Representation 

We adopt the canonical five-element vector representation introduced by Sketch-RNN [23]. A sketch is a sequence of T points, where each point is defined as: 

xt = ( ∆x t, ∆y t, p 1,t , p 2,t , p 3,t ) (1) The first two elements, ∆x t and ∆y t, encode the spatial offset from the previous point. The remaining three elements form a one-hot vector representing the state of the pen: p1,t indicates the pen is touching the paper and drawing a stroke, 

p2,t indicates the pen is lifted to move to a new position, and p3,t marks the end of the drawing. 

3.2 Model Architecture 

The architecture consists of a linear input embedding, a stack of causal selec-tive state-space blocks, and three parallel output heads that share the resulting hidden state. 

Backbone At each step t, a linear layer projects the five-dimensional input xt

into a D-dimensional continuous embedding. This sequence is processed by a Mamba [20] backbone. As a selective SSM, Mamba maps the input sequence to a hidden state sequence ht through a linear ODE whose discretised parameters are data-dependent. This selectivity allows the model to compress the drawing history adaptively, filtering out noisy intermediate movements while retaining structural invariants. Because the recurrence is strictly causal, ht depends only on x1...t .

Task Heads The shared representation ht is passed to three lightweight parallel heads: 1. Classification Head: A linear projection mapping ht to a 58-dimensional logit vector ˆyt, representing the predicted distribution over the Quick, Draw! categories given the prefix up to step t.2. Stroke Spatial Head: Following standard practice for continuous coordi-nate generation [7,23], this head outputs the parameters of a bivariate Gaus-sian mixture model (GMM) with K = 20 components. A linear layer maps

3.3 Dense Per-Step Supervision 

The central methodological contribution of SketchMamba is the dense appli-cation of the classification objective. In standard sequence classification, cross-entropy is computed only at the final step T . We instead supervise the classi-fication head at every valid time step. The total loss is a weighted sum of the recognition and generation objectives:

3.4 Streaming Inference 

At inference time, SketchMamba operates as a true streaming system. When a user draws a stroke, the new point xt is ingested in O(1) time to update the state-space recurrence. The classification head can be queried instantly to retrieve ˆyt, providing a live recognition readout. If generation is required, the spatial and pen heads are sampled to produce ˆxt+1 , which is fed back into the model autoregressively without recalculating the prefix. This dual capacity is enabled entirely by the causal architecture and the dense per-step training regime. 

4 Experiments 

We design our experiments to isolate the impact of the causal backbone and the dense per-step supervision regime. To avoid confounding variables, all models are trained and evaluated within a single unified pipeline. 

4.1 Dataset and Preprocessing 

We use the canonical 58-class Quick, Draw! subset [26,23]. The data is split into 4.06 million training, 145,000 validation, and 145,000 test sketches. We apply Ramer-Douglas-Peucker (RDP) simplification with an epsilon of 2.0 and scale spatial offsets to zero mean and unit variance. Sequences are capped at a maximum length of T = 200 points. 5.1 Matched-Budget Recognition 

SketchMamba achieves 94.93% final-step Top-1 accuracy on the Quick, Draw! test split. In the matched-budget evaluation (Table 3), the Mamba backbone ties a causal Transformer while using 17% fewer parameters. Both models outperform the LSTM, GRU, and CNN1D baselines by margins of 1.5 to 6.6 accuracy points. Under dense per-step supervision, SketchMamba yields a progressive-accuracy Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.706, crossing 90% of its final accuracy by the time 70% of the sketch is drawn. 

5.2 Ablation: The Role of Dense Supervision 

To isolate the driver of early prediction, we ablate the dense per-step classifica-tion loss against a standard final-step-only loss. Training curves (Fig. 2) show both regimes converge to indistinguishable final-step accuracy (Table 4). How-ever, the final-step-only model fails completely at progressive recognition, col-lapsing the AUC from 0.706 to 0.074. The causal architecture alone is insuffi-cient for early prediction; dense supervision is strictly required to structure the intermediate hidden states. 

5.3 Generative Fidelity and Latency 

SketchMamba successfully conditions the bivariate GMM on the same causal state used for recognition, generating semantically appropriate strokes from arbi-trary partial prefixes. Because the SSM updates its state in O(1) time, generation latency remains constant irrespective of prefix length (Fig. 3). 5.4 Discussion and Limitations 

Analysis of the confusion matrix (Fig. 4) reveals structured failure modes. Mis-classifications cluster among visually similar classes (e.g., circles versus clocks, or lines versus swords) where early geometric primitives are identical. Furthermore, the standard cross-entropy loss struggles with pen-state im-balance. Because pen-lift ( p2) and end-of-sketch ( p3) actions account for a small fraction of total sequence steps compared to drawing actions ( p1), the generative head occasionally fails to terminate sketches cleanly. Replacing the uniform pen-state cross-entropy with a focal loss to explicitly up-weight minority transition events represents a direct path for future work. 10 K. Jhaveri et al. 

6 Conclusion 

We introduced SketchMamba, a single causal sequence model that unifies vector-sketch recognition and autoregressive generation. By replacing the standard final-step classification loss with a dense per-step supervision regime, we forced a causal selective state-space backbone to operate as an early-prediction recogniser. The empirical results confirm that this training regime, rather than specialised architectural branching, is the active ingredient for progressive recognition. At 1.55 million parameters, the model matches the final-step accuracy of a causal Transformer while significantly outperforming recurrent and convolutional base-lines, and it processes sequence steps in constant time. Future work will extend this framework to larger vocabularies and address the residual pen-state imbalance using transition-weighted objectives. SketchMamba demonstrates that a single causal hidden state can fully describe a drawing while it is actively being made, directly supporting the requirements of streaming digital ink interfaces.
