Abstract 

Diffusion transformers (DiTs) generate high-fidelity images from text and image conditions, but their outputs carry large variance and their faithfulness to a desired target depends heavily on how the condition is supplied. We present DAGS , a lightweight, attention-free, disentangled appearance and geometry conditioning scheme that steers a frozen image DiT to produce high-fidelity, highly faithful, and independently controllable renders. Two small convo-lutional encoders compute conditioning features once per frame 

and inject them as a learned, per-layer, element-wise residual into the image tokens, avoiding the quadratic cost of stacking condi-tions through attention. Because control and temporal handling live outside the frozen backbone, we retain its vast pretrained prior and eliminate backbone-overfitting risk. 1 Introduction 

Diffusion transformers [Peebles and Xie 2023] generate high-fidelity images, but controlling them precisely remains difficult. The domi-nant control paradigms—reference-image conditioning, semantic conditioning, and input channel stacking—either operate through the attention stack (whose cost scales quadratically with token count) or require coarse-correcting the signal at many layers to stay faithful. ControlNet [Zhang et al. 2023] achieves strong spatial control but duplicates the encoder ( ≈ 50% of the backbone param-eters) and re-injects per-layer features, whereas a small external encoder can already steer a frozen backbone. Semantic features are not the metric geometry a renderer needs, and clip-based video DiTs [Pena et al. 2025] add latency without such handles. We argue that entangled control hurts faithfulness : when a single conditioning stream carries both geometry and appearance, the model is free to be selective about which cue it honors, so structure and color trade off against each other. We instead split control into two disentangled streams and show this improves fidelity and yields independent, predictable control. 

Concurrent work. GateControl [Liu et al. 2026], developed independently, reaches a convergent conclusion for linear-attention 

backbones (SANA): a token-wise gated additive injection—without multimodal attention and leaving text tokens untouched—is a ro-bust, minimal conditioning mechanism. DAGS differs in three re-spects: (i) we steer a full-attention MMDiT (Flux.2), not a linear-attention model; (ii) we use two disentangled encoders for inde-pendent geometry-versus-appearance control; and (iii) we target 

temporally stabilized generative rendering from 1-spp + G-buffers. That the additive, no-cross-attention insight was reached indepen-dently on a different backbone corroborates our design choice. 

Our approach. DAGS steers a frozen Flux.2-Klein 4 B DiT with 

< 10 M trainable parameters via three choices: (i) attention-free, once-per-frame CNN residuals (no token concatenation); (ii) inde-pendent geometry/appearance control (double dissociation, §3.2); 

(iii) temporal stabilization outside the frozen backbone, preserving the full generative prior. 

2 Method 

Overview (Fig. 2). Given a 1-spp path-traced frame plus per-pixel G-buffers (albedo, depth, normal) and a coarse relit prior 

𝑅 (§2.3), DAGS predicts the final rendered frame. Two indepen-dent encoders—geometry and appearance—encode the buffers into token-aligned features that are scaled per DiT layer and added to the image stream. The DiT, its text encoder (fed a fixed static prompt), and the VAE are all frozen. At test time we use ground-truth G-buffers from the same path tracer as the 1-spp frame (no estimated buffers). 

2.1 Geometry encoder 

Two stems process depth and normals independently before their features are fused and jointly refined into geometry condition-ing tokens ( ≈ 4.80 M parameters). Channel/feature dropout dur-ing training makes the encoder compact and robust to missing or noisy buffers. The geometry stream carries sharp edges, occlu-sion boundaries, and surface-orientation cues that pin the structure 

of the output while leaving appearance free to be inferred by the prior. Because depth and normals are deterministic per frame (no Monte-Carlo noise), the geometry stream is comparatively stable and contributes only a small share of temporal inconsistency. 

2.2 Appearance encoder 

The appearance encoder ( ≈ 4.82 M parameters) consumes albedo ,the raw 1-spp frame, and the coarse relit prior 𝑅 —a facetized RGB lighting proxy produced upstream by the recurrent stabilizer (§2.3). Raw 1-spp provides high-frequency lighting—shadows, con-tact darkening, highlights—that 𝑅 smooths away. Temporal flicker is dominated by noisy appearance inputs; we stabilize only 𝑅 (§2.3) plus facetization, leaving raw 1-spp unconstrained, so residual spec-ular flicker remains (§2.6, §4). Together, raw 1-spp and 𝑅 carry lighting; albedo adds stable surface-boundary cues (§3.1).

2.5 Flow source 

Because albedo can cover geometry-conditioning failure modes, we replace the flow-matching source [Lipman et al. 2023] from Gaussian noise with a content-carrying source, learning a shorter transport chord from an approximate image to the fully lit target: we set the source endpoint 𝑥 0 = 𝑅 (the relit prior) rather than 

𝑥 0 ∼ N ( 0, 𝐼 ), shortening the path to the target 𝑥 1 and lowering the conditional variance the few-step ODE must integrate. The same 𝑅 conditions the appearance encoder and initializes the flow. Initializing the flow from the relit prior (the shortest chord) beats noise on LPIPS in all nine scenes and on PSNR in 7 of 9 (up to 

+8.1 dB), and is our best model (Tab. 1). Both learnability and few-step ODE integration error grow with path length and conditional variance; a source that already carries geometry and approximate lighting leaves only a small residual to transport (§3.3).

3 Experiments 

We report PSNR / MS-SSIM / LPIPS, CIE-Lab ΔE2000, depth/normal error, and temporal 𝐸 warp [Lai et al. 2018] / 𝑡 LP [Chu et al. 2020]. §3.1 validates the two-stream design; §3.2 tests independent appearance versus geometry control (Fig. 1); later subsections cover flow source, temporal stabilization (Tab. 1), and OIDN / RGB ↔X baselines. 

3.1 Geometry versus appearance 

Each stream owns a distinct factor and the combination is best. 

Geometry-only yields decent depth but the worst (or near-worst) color (Lab ΔE 6 .9 → 21 .5 if harder; worst in 8 of 9 scenes); appearance-only yields good color but the worst geometry (depth error up to ∼0.6); geometry+appearance inherits both , taking best/tied MS-SSIM+LPIPS+geometry in all nine scenes and best PSNR in 8 of 9 scenes. The two factors are thus complementary and non-competing : adding either stream leaves the other’s axis intact, so the design captures both without a structure-versus-color compro-mise. We also ablate albedo within the appearance-only model 

(geometry encoder disabled): with albedo, inferred depth error falls in 7 of 9 scenes—often by half—while Lab ΔE stays flat. Albedo therefore supplies boundary-aligned structure cues to the ap-pearance stream rather than acting as a relighting knob.

Mechanistic probe. Comparing the geometry and appearance residual embeddings with CKA / RBF-CKA / Gaussian-MI (9 scenes), the raw similarity is almost entirely the trivial positional (shared token-grid) subspace. Once position is removed, cross-branch con-tent similarity is small (CKA ≤ 0.32) and far below within-branch structure (0.95–0.99), though above the shuffled null. We thus claim the branches are disentangled up to a shared positional sub-space, with small residual content overlap —this supports rather than overclaims the behavioral result. 

3.3 Flow source 

We compare three training-time flow sources: Gaussian noise, albedo, and the relit prior 𝑅 . Each is a separate two-stream checkpoint evalu-ated with non-temporal inference (Tab. 1). Relit-flow is strongest: best LPIPS in all nine scenes and best/near-best PSNR in 7 of 9 (mean +2.0 dB over noise). Gains are largest where the transport residual is hardest—up to +8.1 dB on the hardest cases—because 𝑅 

already carries approximate geometry and lighting, shortening the flow chord. Albedo-flow is conditional: it can slightly beat noise on easy interiors but collapses on hard cases (up to −5.0 dB) when the shading gap between albedo and shadowed GT is large. We therefore report relit-flow ≫ noise as the robust claim.

3.5 Comparison 

On the same 1-spp + G-buffer budget we compare to OIDN [Áfra 2019] (training-free denoiser; reconstruction axis) and RGB ↔X

[Zeng et al. 2024] (SD1.5 forward renderer; controllability axis). Tab. 2 and Fig. 3 report quantitative and qualitative results; num-bers are scale-invariant (a per-frame gain removes an exposure confound on reconstruction; temporal metrics match Tab. 1, §3.4; raw numbers widen our lead). DAGS wins reconstruction on 9 of 9 scenes versus both base-lines ( +8.6 dB over OIDN, +10 .1 dB over RGB ↔X, ∼2–3 × lower LPIPS) and perceptual stability ( 𝑡 LP) on 9 of 9 (2 .5× lower than OIDN per-frame, 5 .3× stabilized). Stabilization ( 𝐺 +𝑃 ) trades a small amount of per-frame fidelity for markedly lower flicker. OIDN can-not relight/edit; RGB ↔X adds controllability but, as a per-frame model, flickers badly ( 𝑡 LP ∼8× worse than DAGS-stab) even when fed the same stabilized lighting, and drifts from GT—and its full intrinsic stack (extra roughness and metallic channels that DAGS never sees) changes nothing ( −0.2 dB). This is a matched-input-budget comparison, not matched compute: OIDN is the training-free quality reference, not a speed competitor. 4 Limitations and future work 

DAGS has three main limitations, each pointing to a concrete next step. (i) Domain gap: training uses indoor scenes only ; we eval-uate on held-out and out-of-distribution scenes, but fidelity can drop far from the training distribution—a larger, more diverse cor-pus is the remedy. (ii) Residual flicker: high-frequency detail and specular highlights still flicker where the appearance in-puts are themselves frame-to-frame inconsistent, motivating detail-and specular-aware appearance conditioning. (iii) Motion-regime dependence: under fast camera motion, reprojection validity is low, so guidance cannot beat per-frame inference and the recur-rent stabilizer must be disabled to avoid ghosting. Our temporal handling is external to the frozen backbone (input-space stabilizer + output-space guidance), so it relies on reprojection that breaks under heavy disocclusion; folding temporal feedback into diffusion sampling—conditioning each step on the previous decoded frame while keeping the backbone frozen—could raise this ceiling.
