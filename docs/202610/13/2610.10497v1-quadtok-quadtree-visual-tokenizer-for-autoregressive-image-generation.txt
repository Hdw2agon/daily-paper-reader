## ABSTRACT 

We introduce QuadTok, a novel framework for visual tokenization and autore-gressive image generation. Compared to traditional approaches using 2D grids or 1D token sequences, we propose a hierarchical quadtree structure, bridging the gap between 2D spatial binding and 1D sequence-level flexibility. The Quad-Tok tokenizer dynamically allocates representational capacity to visually intri-cate areas while leaving homogeneous regions at a coarse resolution. Com-pared with a fixed 256-token grid, our ImageNet-trained tokenizer saves ap-proximately 10% of tokens on ImageNet and 9% when transferred zero-shot to COCO dataset, while maintaining comparable reconstruction fidelity. Fur-thermore, the natural causality introduced by the tree structure seamlessly en-ables autoregressive image generation. Conditioned on a quadtree topology sup-plied before generation, our 947M GPT-style generative model achieves a 2.08 

gFID on the ImageNet 256 × 256 benchmark. Additionally, leveraging the strong spatial correlation preserved by the quadtree structure, the QuadTok gen-erator enables zero-shot spatially controlled image generation capabilities. Code:

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Natural images exhibit highly non-uniform spatial information density: regions containing intricate textures and high-frequency patterns carry significantly more information than smooth or homoge-neous areas. Ideally, a visual tokenizer should allocate representational capacity according to this 1arXiv:2610.10497v1 [cs.CV] 7 Oct 2026 Preprint. spatial variability. However, classic visual tokenizers (van den Oord et al., 2018; Sun et al., 2024) compress images into rigid 2D grids with a fixed token budget, distributing tokens uniformly across the image regardless of local complexity. As a result, complex regions are often under-represented while simple regions are over-allocated with redundant tokens. To overcome the rigidity of grid-based tokenization, recent work has explored 1D visual tokeniz-ers (Shen et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2024b; Duggal et al., 2024; Miwa et al., 2025b; Wang et al., 2026; Bachmann et al., 2025b; Chen et al., 2026). These methods model images as sequential token streams and allow for variable length, offering greater flexibility in adapting the token budget to image complexity. Nevertheless, this flexibility comes at the cost of spatial awareness. Because token allocation occurs along the sequence dimension rather than the image plane, these approaches remain largely spatially agnostic. Consequently, they often lack explicit correspondence between tokens and image patches, which can degrade pixel-level fidelity and make them less suitable for downstream tasks that require spatially grounded representations and precise localization. In this work, we propose QuadTok , a novel quadtree-based visual tokenizer that enables adaptive token allocation while preserving explicit spatial correspondence. Instead of relying on a rigid grid or a purely sequential representation, QuadTok represents images using a hierarchical quadtree structure that dynamically subdivides spatial regions based on local complexity. This design bridges the gap between existing paradigms: it retains the spatial grounding of traditional 2D tokenizers while introducing the sequence-level flexibility of 1D token representations. However, one key challenge remains: How can we select which regions to refine based on their reconstruction benefit? 

To tackle this, we propose Region-Wise Complexity Guidance , a data-driven strategy to select a content-adaptive tree for each image based on reconstruction benefit. Furthermore, we identify that maintaining the hierarchical causality and explicit spatial relationships between tree nodes is crucial during the tokenization process. To enforce this structural integrity, we introduce the Kinship Causal Mask . This mechanism dictates a strict coarse-to-fine information flow along the quadtree hierarchy, allowing the global image structure to be established before localized refinement occurs within spatially bounded regions. Empowered by our Region-Wise Complexity Guidance , the QuadTok tokenizer achieves an rFID of 1.46 and a PSNR of 20.37 on ImageNet with 230 tokens on average, saving approximately 10% rel-ative to a fixed 256-token grid. Transferred zero-shot to COCO, the same tokenizer uses 232 tokens on average, saving approximately 9%. Furthermore, leveraging the causality inherent in the quadtree structure, the QuadTok 947M autoregressive generator achieves a gFID of 2.08, compared with 2.15 for the 1.4B RandAR-XXL model (Pang et al., 2025). We also show a consistent scaling behavior on our generator across different model scales. Furthermore, guided by the explicit quadtree topology, QuadTok generator enables novel zero-shot capabilities such as spatially controllable generation. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose QuadTok , a quadtree-based visual tokenizer that replaces fixed-grid represen-tations with spatially adaptive token allocation. Our approach preserves explicit spatial correspondence while enabling flexible token budgets driven by local visual complexity. • We introduce two key mechanisms to support adaptive tokenization: (i) Region-Wise Com-plexity Guidance strategy selecting regional refinement from reconstruction benefit; (ii) a 

Kinship Causal Mask enforcing hierarchical dependencies among tree nodes during tok-enization. • Leveraging the hierarchical causality of quadtrees, QuadTok achieves competitive image generation quality while enabling zero-shot spatially controllable image synthesis condi-tioned on quadtree layouts supplied before generation. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 VISUAL TOKENIZATION 

Visual tokenizers compress images into representations for generative modeling. VQ-VAE (van den Oord et al., 2018) learns discrete codebooks, and VQ-GAN (Esser et al., 2021) adds adversarial training to improve perceptual reconstruction. Later work improves codebook design and tokenizer architectures (Lee et al., 2022; Mentzer et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024a; Zhao et al., 2024). Beyond fixed spatial grids, TiTok (Yu et al., 2024b) represents images as compact 1D sequences. Flex-Tok (Bachmann et al., 2025a) and One-D-Piece (Miwa et al., 2025b) support variable sequence lengths, while GigaTok (Xiong et al., 2025) uses semantic regularization to improve reconstruction 2Preprint. and downstream generation as tokenizers scale. QuadTok focuses on assigning discrete tokens to regions within an image while retaining an explicit spatial correspondence. 

Content-adaptive tokenization. CAT (Shen et al., 2025) adapts representation capacity to image complexity by using caption-derived scores to select an image-wide compression ratio in a nested continuous VAE. Its variable-length latents support image synthesis with a diffusion transformer. QuadTok instead allocates discrete tokens at different spatial resolutions within each image, pre-serving explicit correspondence between tokens and regions. During reconstruction, image content determines which regions to refine; during generation, an autoregressive model predicts visual codes under a topology supplied before sampling. We compare with CAT’s published ImageNet 512 × 512 

generation results in Tab. 7, alongside other high-resolution generators. 2.2 AUTOREGRESSIVE IMAGE GENERATION 

Autoregressive image models differ in both their latent representation and prediction unit (Lee et al., 2022; Li et al., 2024; Shan et al., 2026). LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024) predicts discrete grid tokens sequentially, RandAR (Pang et al., 2025) changes their generation order, and VAR (Tian et al., 2024) predicts successive resolution scales. DPAR (Srivastava et al., 2025) uses an entropy model to group discrete tokens into variable-length patches, reducing the sequence processed by its global transformer. We compare with DPAR as a related approach to spatial token aggregation. QuadTok represents regions directly as quadtree nodes and generates their discrete codes in breadth-first order conditioned on the given tree. Continuous-token methods provide another approach to autoregressive synthesis. xAR (Ren et al., 2025a) generalizes the prediction unit to entities such as cells and uses flow matching with noisy contexts. FlowAR (Ren et al., 2025b) combines scale-wise autoregression with flow matching. QuadTok models variable-length discrete sequences with explicit regional correspondence, allowing generation to be conditioned on a supplied spatial topology. 2.3 QUADTREE REPRESENTATIONS IN VISION 

Quadtrees recursively partition two-dimensional space and have been used for image processing, compression, and adaptive visual computation (Wu, 2018; Tang et al., 2022; Ronen et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2025). QuadTok incorporates this hierarchy into a generative visual tokenizer. Its topology-aware attention preserves structured interactions among tokens, and its discrete represen-tation supports image reconstruction and autoregressive synthesis under a supplied spatial topology. 

## 3 METHOD 

3.1 QUAD TOK TOKENIZER 

We introduce QuadTok, a visual tokenizer that represents images using an adaptive quadtree struc-ture. Unlike conventional methods that rely on fixed grids, QuadTok dynamically allocates tokens across regions while maintaining explicit correspondence between tokens and the image patches. 3.1.1 QUADTREE REPRESENTATION 

QuadTok represents an image using a hierarchical quadtree decomposition, where each node corre-sponds to a spatial region and is treated as a visual token. A node is defined by the attribute tuple 

(l, p ), where l denotes the tree level and p the spatial index. Level l = 1 represents coarse regions of the image, while level l = 2 corresponds to higher-resolution subdivisions. Each parent node may expand into four child nodes covering its spatial quadrants. Our default configuration uses two levels; we also evaluate a three-level extension in Secs. 4.1 and 4.2. To interface this hierarchical structure with Transformer architecture, the quadtree is serialized into a 1D sequence using breadth-first search (BFS) traversal. This ordering first lists all coarse tokens and then their corresponding higher-resolution child tokens, naturally aligning with coarse-to-fine generation. 3.1.2 IMAGE ENCODING

Although the tokenizer can operate on arbitrary quadtree structures, training with a fixed fully ex-panded tree would cause the model to rely on a deterministic token layout. To improve robustness to variable tree structures, we employ a probabilistic tree sampling strategy during training. Instead of always using a fully expanded quadtree, we randomly sample valid subtrees for each training iteration. All level-1 nodes are always retained to preserve the global image layout, while each parent node independently expands into its four child nodes with probability γ. When a parent node is not expanded, its spatial region remains represented solely by the coarse-level token. This stochastic sampling exposes the tokenizer to diverse tree configurations and sequence lengths during training. As a result, the model learns to produce consistent representations under both coarse and refined token layouts, enabling it to robustly process the adaptive tree structures used during complexity-guided tokenization (to be introduced in Sec. 3.3). 3.2 KINSHIP CAUSAL ATTENTION 

Flattening the quadtree into a 1D sequence removes the explicit hierarchical constraints of the orig-inal structure. Without additional restrictions, standard self-attention would allow tokens to interact globally, potentially breaking the spatial correspondence between tokens and their associated image regions. To preserve the hierarchical topology, we introduce Kinship Causal Attention , a topology-aware masking strategy that constrains attention according to the quadtree structure. During encoding, the Aggregator Transformer processes the concatenated sequence [F ∥ Q] of ViT feature tokens F and quadtree instruction tokens Q. Attention is asymmetric: ViT tokens attend bidirectionally among themselves, and quadtree tokens attend to all ViT tokens, but not vice versa. Tree tokens thus draw on globally contextualized image features, while their region and level embeddings specify the regions they represent. During decoding, the Decoder Transformer processes the quantized visual tokens together with their corresponding quadtree instruction tokens. In both the encoding and decoding stages, the self-attention interactions among quadtree tokens are governed by our proposed Kinship Causal Mask ,which enforces a strict coarse-to-fine dependency consistent with the quadtree hierarchy. This mask is used within the tokenizer, whereas the autoregressive generator uses a standard causal mask over the BFS token sequence. For the two-level tokenizer, the Kinship Causal Mask follows three structural rules: • Parent ↔ Parent. Coarse-level tokens attend to each other bidirectionally to establish the global image layout. • Child → Parent. Fine-level tokens attend to all parent tokens to obtain global structural context, while parent tokens are prevented from attending to child tokens. • Child ↔ Sibling. Fine-level tokens attend only to other child tokens that share the same parent node, restricting refinement within each spatial region. Fig. 3 illustrates the two-level pattern. The same rule extends to deeper trees: a node can attend to all nodes at coarser levels and to siblings sharing its immediate parent at the same level, but not to finer-level nodes. The coarsest level retains full bidirectional attention. Thus, level-3 nodes attend to all level-1 and level-2 nodes and bidirectionally to their level-3 siblings. These constraints impose a coarse-to-fine flow and restrict same-level interactions among tree tokens to siblings. The ablation in Sec. 4.4.1 evaluates their effect on reconstruction and generation. 3.3 REGION -WISE COMPLEXITY GUIDED TOKENIZATION

Given a quadtree structure T supplied before generation, we synthesize its visual token sequence 

Y = [ y1, · · · , y M ] autoregressively: p(Y |c, T ) = QMi=1 p(yi|y<i , c, t ≤i) where c denotes the con-ditioning signal ( e.g . a class label) and ti = ( li, p i) represents the tree attributes of node i. The topology is selected before sampling, but node instructions are presented in BFS order. At step i,the model can use only the current and preceding node instructions, not future nodes. We parameterize this distribution using a LLaMA (Touvron et al., 2023)-style decoder-only Trans-former with a standard causal attention mask. Each visual-token prediction depends only on pre-ceding visual codes and the supplied conditioning information; the tokenizer’s bidirectional parent and sibling interactions are not used in the generator. Structural instruction embeddings encode the quadtree geometry, which specifies both the tree level and spatial position of each token, enabling the model to condition generation on spatial hierarchy. During inference, tokens are generated sequentially in BFS order. Coarse tokens appear first, estab-lishing the global structure, after which finer tokens progressively refine local details and textures. The generator predicts visual codes under the supplied topology, enabling layout-controlled synthe-sis from user-defined or algorithmically constructed trees.

## 5 CONCLUSION 

We present QuadTok, a quadtree-based tokenizer that enables spatially adaptive, variable-length vi-sual representations. By organizing tokens in a quadtree structure, QuadTok allocates tokens accord-ing to local complexity while preserving spatial correspondence between tokens and image regions which bridges adaptive tokenization and structured spatial representations. Experiments show that QuadTok achieves strong reconstruction and generation performance while enabling zero-shot spa-tial layout control. The overall training objective Ltotal is formulated as a weighted sum of four distinct loss compo-nents: 

Ltotal = λM SE LM SE + λLP IP S LLP IP S + λV Q LV Q + λGAN LGAN 

where LM SE denotes the Mean Squared Error for exact pixel-level fidelity, and LLP IP S is the Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity loss, which preserves high-frequency visual details and structural integrity. To further enhance the perceptual realism and sharpness of the reconstructed images, we incorporate an adversarial GAN loss, denoted by LGAN . The hyperparameters λM SE , λLP IP S , λV Q , and λGAN control the relative contribution of each corresponding objective. ## C QUAD TOK GENERATOR TRAINING DETAILS 

The architecture of the QuadTok generator follows the decoder-only Autoregressive(AR) generation paradigm adopted in LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024). We incorporate several modern architectural components, including Query-Key (QK) Normalization (Henry et al., 2020), SwiGLU activation functions (Shazeer, 2020), and Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) (Heo et al., 2024). We scale the two-level generator across four model sizes: 111M (B), 343M (L), 697M (XL), and 947M (XXL) parameters. The three-level generator contains 344M parameters. All models are trained for 300 epochs (approximately 360K iterations) using a global batch size of 1024 , without apply-ing exponential moving average (EMA) to the model weights. Optimization is performed using the AdamW (Kingma & Ba, 2014) optimizer with β1 = 0 .9, β2 = 0 .95 , and weight decay 0.05 .The learning rate is set to 4 × 10 −4 for the first 250 epochs without warm-up, followed by a linear decay to 1 × 10 −5 over the final 50 epochs. To mitigate overfitting and enable Classifier-Free Guid-ance (Ho & Salimans, 2022b;a) (CFG), the class conditioning embeddings are randomly dropped with probability 0.1 during training. During inference, we employ a standard Key-Value (KV) cache to accelerate autoregressive de-coding, together with a dynamic power-cosine CFG scheduler to improve sample fidelity. A key characteristic of our framework is that the quadtree topology must be predefined before generation. 

Quadtree structure during inference. For class-conditional ImageNet evaluation, we sample quadtree topologies independently of image content using a probabilistic expansion policy. Each sampled topology specifies the regions to refine, and its node instructions are presented in breadth-first order as the generator predicts visual codes.

## F SPATIAL LAYOUT EVALUATION DETAILS 

We evaluate the frozen two-level ImageNet-trained generator and tokenizer using CFG scale 6, a power-cosine guidance schedule with exponent 1, and sampling temperature 1. Each condition contains 20 images for each of 50 fixed ImageNet classes, giving 1,000 images per condition. The four prescribed layouts correspond to the left, right, top, and bottom half-planes. Each topology retains all nodes of an 8 × 8 coarse grid and expands the 32 cells in the target half-plane, adding four children per cell for a total of 192 visual tokens. The matched random baseline uniformly selects exactly 32 coarse cells for expansion independently for each image. Its generated image set is shared across the four directional evaluations. Grad-CAM uses the intended class in ResNet-50. Box localization uses the highest-confidence intended-object box from Grounding DINO, and SAM 2 receives that box as its mask prompt. The Grad-CAM center of mass, box center, or mask centroid is tested against the requested half-plane. Box and mask scores count missed detections as failures and use all generated images as the denom-inator. FID-50cls compares Inception-v3 features of the 1,000 generated images in each condition with the same 2,500 ImageNet validation images from the 50 evaluated classes. Its value is 35.03 for the random-topology baseline and 35.15, 34.88, 34.97, and 35.05 for the prescribed left, right, top, and bottom layouts, respectively.

We have not yet scaled the framework to massive, open-domain datasets (e.g., LAION) nor extended the architecture to encompass complex text-to-image synthesis paradigms. Scaling the model ca-pacity and extending the cross-attention mechanisms for open-vocabulary text conditioning require significantly more training compute and represent a critical next step. 

Dataset Bias in Layout Control. Second, the inherent bias of the ImageNet dataset towards object-centric, single-subject compositions restricts the upper bound of our zero-shot spatial layout control. Although QuadTok effectively localizes primary subjects and separates them from the background based on topological priors, synthesizing highly complex, multi-object scenes with intricate rela-tional layouts remains challenging. The model’s structural prior is naturally limited by the compo-sitional diversity of its training data. We leave the scaling of QuadTok to large-scale text-to-image generation and its application to scene-level dataset for complex, multi-object layout control to future work. 19
