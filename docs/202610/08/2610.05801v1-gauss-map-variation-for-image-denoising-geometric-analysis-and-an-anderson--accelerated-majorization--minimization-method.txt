GAUSS-MAP VARIATION FOR IMAGE DENOISING: GEOMETRIC ANALYSIS AND AN ANDERSON–ACCELERATED MAJORIZATION–MINIMIZATION METHOD 

HAIBIN SU ∗

Abstract. We propose a Gauss-map variation (GMV) model for image denoising that measures the spatial variation of the tangent-plane projectors of the scaled image graph. We establish an equivalent representation of the regularizer in terms of the corresponding Gauss map and, using differential geometric tools including tubular coordinates and the Frenet frame, analyze its behavior across general C2 and piecewise C2 boundaries. The resulting estimates provide edge- and corner-contrast preservation properties. To solve the proposed model, we introduce a bilinear decomposition involving a unit normal field and a scalar magnitude field and develop an Anderson-accelerated majorization–minimization algorithm. The normal field subproblem admits an explicit pointwise majorization–minimization update, which is combined with an Anderson acceleration. For both L1

and L2 data fidelity terms, we establish sufficient decrease and boundedness of the iterates and prove that the generated sequence converges to a critical point of the penalized model. Numerical experiments on synthetic and natural images demonstrate the boundary preserving capability of the proposed model and its competitive performance in removing Gaussian and impulsive noise. 

Key words. Gauss-map variation, geometric regularization, bilinear decomposition, majorization-minimization method, Anderson acceleration, image denoising

1. Introduction. Image denoising aims to recover a clean image from noisy ob-servations. Recovering fine structures is particularly challenging because noise and meaningful image features may occupy overlapping pixels. Edges, corners, and inten-sity contrasts encode object boundaries and shape information, and their preservation is essential to a faithful reconstruction. Variational methods address this problem by combining a data fidelity term with a regularizer that expresses prior information. The central modeling challenge is to suppress noise while retaining these geometric features, rather than treating all intensity variations as undesirable oscillations. The total variation model [18] effectively removes noise while preserving sharp edges [20]. However, it may produce staircase artifacts, reduce image contrast, and smooth corners [6, 9, 17, 2]. High order models [6, 26, 16, 4] alleviate staircasing and better recover smoothly varying regions. Nevertheless, they may oversmooth sharp structures, thereby blurring object boundaries and corners. Geometry based models provide a complementary approach by regularizing geo-metric quantities associated with image level sets or the image graph. Representa-tive examples include Euler’s elastica model [19, 23, 34, 7, 14, 22], mean curvature based model [32, 31, 28], Gaussian curvature model [5, 12], and Weingarten-map based model [29]. Related curvature based regularizers have also been investigated in [30, 25, 27, 15]. The total scaled-gradient variation (TSGV) framework [24, 21] instead penalizes the variation of a nonlinearly scaled image gradient. These mod-els incorporate geometric information, such as local orientation and bending, that is not fully represented by derivative magnitudes alone. This geometric dependence allows them to accommodate sharp object boundaries while suppressing oscillations in smooth regions. The preservation of edges, corners, and intensity contrast has also motivated

8. Conclusion. In this paper, we proposed a Gauss-map variation model that exploits the tangent plane geometry of the scaled image graph and analyzed its behav-ior near general C2 and piecewise C2 boundaries, using differential geometric tools. To solve the resulting model, we developed an Anderson-accelerated majorization– minimization algorithm based on a bilinear decomposition. The normal field subprob-lem admits an explicit MM update, while a depth-one Anderson acceleration improves its practical efficiency without sacrificing energy descent. We proved that the gener-ated sequence has finite length and converges to a critical point of the constrained penalized objective. Numerical experiments demonstrated the boundary preserving capability of the proposed model, the monotone decay of the objective energy, and its competitive performance for Gaussian and impulsive noise removal.
