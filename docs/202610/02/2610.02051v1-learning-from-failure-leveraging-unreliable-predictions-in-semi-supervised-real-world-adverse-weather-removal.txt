## ABSTRACT 

Adverse weather image restoration aims to recover images degraded by rain, haze, snow, and other weather-induced artifacts, thereby improving the robustness of outdoor vision systems. Existing unified restoration models exhibit limited gener-alization to real-world scenes due to their reliance on synthetic supervision and in-sufficient semantic constraints. In this paper, we propose a novel student–teacher semi-supervised framework that addresses both challenges. Specifically, we in-troduce an unreliable database that preserves failed teacher predictions as infor-mative negative samples for contrastive learning, while a reliable database stores high-quality teacher predictions as positive samples. By jointly exploiting reli-able pseudo-ground truths and unreliable teacher outputs, the proposed frame-work learns to enhance desirable restoration characteristics while avoiding com-mon failures. We further propose a phase spectrum-based semantic constraint that replaces computationally expensive text-based supervision with an efficient and naturally aligned semantic prior. An adaptive phase consistency loss is also de-signed to dynamically balance supervision between the degraded input and teacher pseudo-ground truths according to degradation severity. Extensive experiments on real-world benchmarks demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outper-forms existing state-of-the-art approaches in restoration quality and perceptual fi-delity while exhibiting stronger generalization to real-world adverse weather con-ditions. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Images captured in real-world environments are often degraded by adverse weather conditions such as rain, haze, and snow, which significantly deteriorate the performance of outdoor vision systems. Existing image restoration methods have mainly focused on task-specific models tailored to individ-ual weather phenomena (Wang et al., 2025b). Although these methods achieve strong performance through extensive training in large-scale synthetic datasets, their highly specialized designs limit scalability in diverse weather conditions. To address this limitation, recent studies have proposed unified restoration frameworks capable of handling multiple weather degradations within a single model (Wang et al., 2025a; Wu et al., 2026). Although these approaches demonstrate promising results on synthetic benchmarks, they exhibit limited generalization to real-world scene. We attribute this to two key factors: their heavy reliance on synthetic supervision and neglect of the semantic context of the scenes, due to insufficient con-straints during training. To alleviate the dependency on synthetic data, unsupervised restoration methods have been proposed that require only unpaired real-world images (Liu et al., 2025a). However, these approaches fail to exploit the abundant labeled synthetic datasets that have become widely available. Semi-supervised learning offers a more attractive compromise by leveraging both labeled synthetic and unlabeled real-world data. For example, WResVLM (Xu et al., 2024) adopts a student–teacher framework in which the teacher generates pseudo-ground truths (pseudo-GTs) for unlabeled images. Neverthe-less, the framework performs contrastive learning using only positive samples derived from reliable 1arXiv:2610.02051v1 [cs.CV] 1 Oct 2026 pseudo-ground truths, overlooking informative negative samples that could explicitly guide the stu-dent away from poor restoration results. We observe that the teacher frequently produces low-quality predictions during training. These predictions often contain severe artifacts, color distortions, or in-complete restoration (Fig. 6b), and have previously been simply discarded. However, we note that they are not just generic negative samples, but are in fact sampled directly from the model in an 

on-policy manner, and hence provide valuable information (Tang et al., 2024). To address the pre-vious limitation, we propose a mechanism that leverages these informative failures for contrastive semi-supervised learning. More specifically, we retain these cases in an unreliable database and treat them as negative samples for contrastive learning. By simultaneously attracting the student to-ward reliable restorations while repelling it from unreliable ones, the proposed framework provides stronger supervision and improves generalization to real-world adverse weather conditions. Besides the supervision strategy, effective semantic guidance is equally important for restoring vi-sually plausible images. Recent methods employ large vision-language models (VLMs) to generate textual descriptions that explicitly constrain image semantics (Xu et al., 2024). Although effective, text supervision suffers from two inherent drawbacks. First, textual descriptions are only weakly aligned with the fine-grained spatial structures required for low-level vision tasks because of lan-guage ambiguity and the lossy language-vision representation gap (Wu et al., 2024). Second, gener-ating accurate descriptions with large VLMs introduces substantial computational overhead, slowing the training process. Instead of relying on language supervision, we exploit the Fourier phase spectrum as an efficient structural prior. It is well established that the Fourier phase spectrum primarily preserves structural and semantic information, whereas the amplitude spectrum mainly represents low-level appearance (Oppenheim et al., 1979). Existing Fourier-based restoration methods (Guo et al., 2022) mainly focus on recovering degraded amplitude information. In contrast, we employ the phase spectrum to constrain structural consistency during semi-supervised learning. Even though the pseudo-GTs are accessible, using them alone is not optimal. (Oppenheim & Lim, 1980) shows that the phase spectrum is inherently robust to illumination variations and color shifts, making it possible to also use the phase spectrum of the input image to constrain the structure of the output image. Neverthe-less, severe weather degradations may also corrupt phase information by obscuring structural details (Liu et al., 2025a). Therefore, directly enforcing consistency with the phase spectrum of either the degraded input or pseudo-GT can lead to sub-optimal solutions. To overcome this issue, we propose an adaptive phase consistency loss that dynamically balances supervision between the degraded input and the teacher-generated pseudo-GT according to degradation severity. Consequently, the model relies more on the input phase for mild degradations while progressively shifting toward the pseudo-GT phase under severe degradations. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose a new mechanism in the family of semi-supervised weather removal methods that identifies and preserves failed teacher predictions in an unreliable database, enabling their use as negative samples for contrastive semi-supervised learning and improving gen-eralization to real-world adverse weather image restoration. • We introduce the Fourier phase spectrum as an efficient structural prior, eliminating the need for computationally expensive and ambiguous text-based supervision. • We propose an adaptive phase consistency loss that automatically adjusts structural super-vision according to degradation severity, producing more robust restorations across diverse adverse weather conditions. • The proposed framework establishes new state-of-the-art performance among unified and semi-supervised image restoration methods, outperforming existing approaches on both quantitative and qualitative evaluation. 

## 2 RELATED WORKS 

2.1 TASK -SPECIFIC IMAGE RESTORATION 

Image restoration is a fundamental problem in computer vision, aiming to recover high-quality im-ages from degraded observations. Early task-specific methods (Jiang et al., 2017; Ancuti & Ancut , i, 2Figure 1: The illustration of the proposed semi-supervised learning framework. 2013) primarily relied on physical imaging models and handcrafted priors tailored to individual degradation types. With the advent of convolutional neural networks (Yang et al., 2024; 2023) and, more recently, transformer-based architectures have significantly advanced restoration performance across a wide range of tasks. For example, Restormer (Zamir et al., 2021) introduces an efficient multi-scale attention mechanism that achieves an excellent balance between restoration quality and computational efficiency. These transformer-based architectures have been successfully applied to deraining (Chen et al., 2023), desnowing (Zhang et al., 2022), and dehazing (Song et al., 2023). De-spite their impressive performance, most existing methods remain task-specific and require separate models for different degradation types, leading to increased training, storage, and deployment costs. 2.2 ALL -IN -O NE IMAGE RESTORATION 

To mitigate the inefficiency of deploying multiple models for diverse weather degradations, unified image restoration methods have attracted increasing attention (Zhu et al., 2023; Valanarasu et al., 2021). While these approaches achieve impressive performance on synthetic benchmarks, they of-ten struggle in real-world scenarios due to the substantial domain gap between synthetic and real-world degradations. To bridge this gap, (Liu et al., 2025b) proposed a self-evolving framework that combines physics-driven synthetic data generation with a dual-level reinforcement learning strat-egy. Meanwhile, (Tang et al., 2026) addressed the problem in an unsupervised setting employing global and local discriminators to enhance image realism while preserving structural consistency. WResVLM (Xu et al., 2024) introduced a semi-supervised framework that leverages both labeled synthetic data and unlabeled real-world data through VLM-based semantic supervision. Although effective, the reliance on large VLMs incurs substantial computational overhead and limits scalabil-ity and adaptability to resource-constrained applications. we observe that the Fourier phase spectrum inherently preserves the structural and semantic information of an image while being significantly more computationally efficient than VLM-based representations. 3.1 OVERALL FRAMEWORK 

Motivated by the observations that existing semi-supervised image restoration methods lack infor-mative negative samples and that VLM-based semantic constraints are computationally expensive, we propose a novel semi-supervised framework that addresses both limitations. Specifically, we incorporate an unreliable image database into the unsupervised learning process to provide chal-lenging negative samples, while replacing conventional VLM-based semantic supervision with a lightweight phase spectrum-based semantic constraint. This design improves both training effec-tiveness and computational efficiency. As illustrated in Fig. 1, our method follows the conventional student–teacher paradigm widely adopted in semi-supervised learning (Sohn et al., 2020). The framework consists of a student net-work fθs and a teacher network fθt sharing the same architecture. Ideally, the teacher network produces higher-quality predictions than the student network and there-fore provides reliable supervisory signals for unlabeled data. To improve the quality of pseudo-GTs, other methods (Huang et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024) retain only reliable teacher outputs, where relia-bility is assessed using the no-reference image quality assessment (NR-IQA) metric. However, this assumption does not always hold in practice. As shown in Fig. 6, the teacher may still generate un-satisfactory predictions containing severe artifacts, color distortion, or only marginal improvements over the degraded input. Such erroneous outputs are simply discarded in existing methods, leaving their valuable negative information unexplored. To exploit these failure cases, we introduce an unreliable database that explicitly stores the best historical failure for each unlabeled sample. Instead of treating poor predictions as useless, we regard them as informative negative samples that indicate undesirable restoration outcomes. During training, the student network is encouraged to avoid producing outputs similar to those stored in the unreliable database, while the teacher network inherits this behavior through EMA updates. Specifically, we initialize both the reliable database and the unreliable database as empty. For each unlabeled image xui ∈ DU = {xui }Mi=1 , the current teacher prediction is compared with the degraded input, the student prediction, and the historical entries in both databases. If the teacher prediction achieves the highest image quality, it replaces the corresponding entry in the reliable database. Oth-erwise, if it represents a failed prediction but is of higher quality than the existing entry in the unreliable database, the unreliable database is updated accordingly. Restoring images degraded by adverse weather requires not only recovering visual fidelity but also preserving the underlying structures obscured by weather-induced artifacts. Semantic supervision is particularly beneficial in semi-supervised learning, where reliable labels are unavailable for real-world data. To exploit semantic information, WResVLM (Xu et al., 2024) employs VLMs to con-struct a contrastive semantic supervision objective. Although effective, such approaches incur sub-stantial computational overhead and rely on language representations that are inherently ambiguous and weakly aligned with the fine-grained spatial structures required for low-level vision tasks. Instead, we propose a lightweight phase spectrum-based structural constraint. Our design is mo-tivated by two observations. First, previous studies have shown that the Fourier phase spectrum preserves most of the semantic and structural information of an image (Guo et al., 2022). Second, although the phase spectrum is generally robust to illumination changes and color shifts, severe degradations may still corrupt its structural information (Liu et al., 2025a). Consequently, neither the degraded input nor the teacher-generated pseudo-GT should be treated as an absolutely reliable structural reference. To address this issue, we construct an adaptive phase consistency loss by combining the phase spec-trum of the degraded input and the reliable pseudo-GT according to their estimated image qualities. ## 5 CONCLUSION 

This paper presented an unreliable-aware semi-supervised learning framework for real-world ad-verse weather image restoration. The proposed framework combines an unreliable bank for ex-ploiting failed pseudo-GTs as hard negative samples with an adaptive phase consistency loss that replaces expensive vision-language model-based semantic supervision. Together, these components effectively reduce confirmation bias while providing efficient structural guidance during training. Future work will investigate extending the implementation.
