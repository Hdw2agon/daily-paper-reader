# Unsupervised Maneuver-Aware Acoustic Fault Detection for Autonomous Drones 

Ali M Ali, Ziyi Tang, Nurdaulet Nazarbay, and Hashim A. Hashim 

Abstract —This paper presents a maneuver-aware acoustic fault detection framework for autonomous drones that integrates Noise2Noise-inspired deep learning denoising with maneuver-conditioned reconstruction. A key practical constraint motivating this work is that labeled faulty-flight data are difficult and potentially unsafe to collect; the proposed framework therefore follows an unsupervised learning paradigm in which only nominal flight recordings are required for training. In flight environments, acoustic signals acquired from unmanned aerial vehicles are sub-ject to variability arising both from environmental noise and from structured, maneuver-dependent aerodynamic effects. To address these challenges simultaneously, a two-stage learning architecture is developed. In the first stage, a Noise2Noise-inspired denoising model attenuates stochastic acoustic noise while preserving fault-relevant spectral-temporal structures, without requiring clean reference signals. In the second stage, a maneuver-Conditioned Convolutional AutoEncoder (maneuver-CCAE) is trained using maneuver-related labels including drone type and flight direction to model nominal acoustic behavior under varying operating conditions. Fault detection is subsequently performed using reconstruction error as an anomaly score. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed maneuver-aware conditioning raises the area under the ROC curve (AUC) from 0.561 (un-conditioned baseline) to 0.884 (full model), validating the critical role of maneuver-dependent modeling. The complete framework is deployed on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super embedded platform within a ROS2 pipeline, achieving an end-to-end fault detection latency of approximately 20 ms per audio segment with a TensorRT half-precision (FP16) backend, confirming real-time viability for onboard UAV health monitoring. 

Index Terms —Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), acoustic fault detection, unsupervised anomaly detection, Noise2Noise-inspired denoising, conditional autoencoder, maneuver-conditioned learn-ing, embedded real-time inference.

Early acoustic fault detection approaches were largely based on deterministic signal processing, exploiting time-domain statistics, spectral features, and time-frequency representations to identify deviations from nominal behavior [2]–[6]. R ELATED WORK 

A. Deterministic Signal-Processing Based Acoustic FDD 

Prior to the widespread adoption of machine learning, acoustic FDD relied predominantly on deterministic, physics-based signal processing. These methods detect faults by mon-itoring changes in time-domain statistics (e.g., Root Mean Square (RMS) energy, kurtosis, crest factor), frequency-domain signatures (e.g., power spectral density), or joint time-frequency representations [2]–[5]. Envelope analysis and cepstrum-based techniques are particularly effective for iden-tifying periodic impulsive faults, such as bearing damage or gear mesh anomalies [4], [5]. To address speed vari-ability, order tracking methods transform frequency spectra into speed-normalized representations [6], [17]. In Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) applications, lightweight Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)-based monitoring and spectral thresholding have been employed for detecting propeller imbalance and motor faults [18], [19]. While deterministic methods offer interpretability and low computational cost, their performance is often sensitive to noise, recording conditions, and maneuver-induced aerodynamic variability. 

B. Supervised Learning for Acoustic Fault Diagnosis 

Supervised learning approaches formulate fault diagnosis as a classification problem using labeled examples of healthy and faulty states. Traditional machine learning pipelines typ-ically rely on handcrafted features such as Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC), spectral entropy, and time-frequency descriptors, combined with classifiers including Support Vector Machine (SVM), decision trees, 𝑘 -Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and ensemble methods [9], [10], [20], [21]. Deep learning has significantly advanced supervised acoustic FDD by enabling end-to-end learning from raw waveforms or spectrogram representations [11], [22], [23]. CNN-based models have demonstrated strong performance in detecting propeller damage and rotor impairments [24]. Recurrent archi-tectures including Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), and Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) networks effectively capture temporal dependencies [25]–[27]. Despite these advances, supervised approaches face fundamen-tal scalability limitations: acquiring labeled fault data typically requires artificially inducing damage, which is costly and potentially unsafe, and models may generalize poorly to new operating conditions. 

C. Unsupervised Anomaly Detection and Reconstruction-Based Methods 

Unsupervised anomaly detection mitigates the need for la-beled fault data by identifying deviations from nominal behav-ior [28]. Reconstruction-based anomaly detection has emerged as a dominant paradigm for acoustic monitoring. Autoencoders and their variants, including convolutional, variational, denois-ing, and masked autoencoders, learn compact representations of healthy signals and use reconstruction error as an anomaly score [13]–[16], [29]. Such methods have been successfully applied to UAV propeller monitoring [13], wind turbine di-agnostics [14], [30], and industrial machinery fault detection using benchmark datasets such as Malfunctioning Industrial Machine Investigation and Inspection (MIMII) [15]. To im-prove generalization, recent studies have explored lightweight autoencoder architectures for embedded deployment [29] and conditional or hierarchical models that incorporate auxiliary information into the reconstruction process [16]. These results suggest that explicitly modeling structured sources of variabil-ity can significantly enhance anomaly detection performance. 

D. Maneuver-Dependent Variability: An Open Challenge 

A persistent limitation across much of the acoustic FDD literature is the inadequate treatment of maneuver-dependent variability in drone acoustics. Maneuvers alter aerodynamic loading, flow interaction, and propulsion dynamics, producing systematic changes in acoustic emissions even under healthy operation. When such effects are not explicitly modeled, they can be misinterpreted as anomalies, leading to false alarms and reduced operational reliability. While some supervised multi-class systems have incorporated maneuver information [31], maneuver conditioning remains largely unexplored in unsu-pervised reconstruction-based frameworks. This gap motivates the present work. 3

E. Contributions 

Unlike deterministic signal-processing approaches [32], [33], the proposed framework does not require fixed spectral signatures or threshold tuning and remains effective under highly non-stationary flight conditions. Unlike supervised methods [9], [27], it operates in an unsupervised manner and requires only nominal flight data for training. Although recent works [34], [35] have demonstrated effective deep learning-based acoustic anomaly detection, most consider stationary or quasi-stationary operating conditions. UAV acoustic signals exhibit significant non-stationarity due to maneuver-dependent aerodynamic effects, motivating the need for maneuver-conditioned diagnostic models. The contributions of this paper are as follows: 1) UAV acoustic fault detection is formulated as a maneuver-conditioned unsupervised learning task, in which nominal acoustic behavior is explicitly modeled as a function of maneuver-related operating conditions (e.g., drone type and flight direction). This enables a systematic separa-tion of maneuver-induced variability from fault-induced acoustic deviations and is, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, the first such formulation for UAV acoustic fault detection. 2) A two-stage architecture integrates Noise2Noise-inspired acoustic denoising with a conditional convolutional au-toencoder. The denoising stage attenuates stochastic environmental disturbances using maneuver-consistent surrogate pairs, without requiring clean reference sig-nals, while the conditional reconstruction stage captures maneuver-dependent nominal dynamics through Condi-tional Batch Normalization (CBN). 3) The complete pipeline, training, threshold selection, and inference, uses only healthy-flight acoustic data. The anomaly threshold is fixed at the 95th percentile of the nominal validation reconstruction-error distribution, so no faulty sample or label is examined at any stage. 4) The framework is deployed and evaluated on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super embedded platform within a ROS2 pipeline, with the two networks exported to ONNX and executed as TensorRT half-precision (FP16) engines on the GPU Tensor Cores. This allows larger-capacity networks to run at an end-to-end latency of ≈ 20 ms per audio segment, confirming practical real-time viability for onboard UAV health monitoring. III. P ROBLEM FORMULATION 

This section formulates the acoustic anomaly detection problem considered in this work for quadrotor drones. The objective is to detect abnormal mechanical conditions, such as motor degradation or propeller damage, using acoustic signals recorded by an onboard microphone during flight. A key practical constraint of the problem is that only healthy-flight data are available during training, since collecting labeled faulty data can be costly and unsafe. Therefore, the proposed framework follows an unsupervised learning approach, where the model learns the nominal acoustic behavior of the drone under different maneuvering conditions. During operation, deviations from the learned nominal acoustic model are quan-tified using an anomaly score derived from the reconstruction error. This anomaly score allows the system to differentiate abnormal acoustic signatures caused by mechanical faults from normal maneuver-induced acoustic variations.

The choice of reconstruction loss as the anomaly scoring function is motivated by the strictly unsupervised constraint of this work. In the absence of labeled faulty data during training, no class-discriminative loss can be optimized directly. Recon-struction loss provides a principled self-supervised surrogate: the model is trained to compress and reconstruct nominal signals accurately, so that any input deviating from the learned nominal manifold will incur a higher reconstruction error. A higher reconstruction error indicates a greater deviation from the learned nominal acoustic manifold and is therefore more likely to correspond to a fault condition. This property is par-ticularly well suited to log-mel spectrogram representations, where fault-induced deviations manifest as localized changes in spectral energy distribution. Alternative loss functions such as contrastive or metric learning losses require negative (faulty) samples during training and are therefore incompatible with the unsupervised constraint. Adversarial losses introduce training instability that is undesirable in resource-constrained embedded deployment. The hybrid MSE+SSIM formulation adopted here (see Eq. (16)) penalizes both pointwise spectral differences and structural dissimilarity, preserving harmonic patterns important for acoustic fault characterization.

IV. P ROPOSED ARCHITECTURE 

This section presents the proposed two-stage learning ar-chitecture for maneuver-aware acoustic fault detection in au-tonomous drones. The framework addresses two fundamental challenges: (i) strong stochastic disturbances arising from environmental noise and wind effects, and (ii) structured, maneuver-dependent variability in nominal acoustic emissions. First, a Noise2Noise-inspired denoising network suppresses stochastic acoustic corruption without requiring clean refer-ence signals. Second, a maneuver-conditioned convolutional autoencoder models nominal acoustic behavior and detects faults through reconstruction error.

Remark 1. Unlike the original Noise2Noise formulation, which assumes two independently corrupted realizations of the same underlying clean signal, the proposed implementation employs maneuver-consistent surrogate targets constructed from samples belonging to the same operating condition. As a result, the independence assumption of the original Noise2Noise theory is not strictly satisfied. Since true indepen-dent noise realizations of the same underlying signal are not available for in-flight drone recordings, maneuver-consistent surrogate pairs are used as training targets, following the spirit of the Noise2Noise paradigm under the assumption that the clean spectral-temporal structure remains approximately stable within the same maneuver condition. The proposed denoising stage should therefore be interpreted as a weak-reference Noise2Noise-inspired learning strategy that lever-ages structured similarity within maneuver classes. The objec-tive is not to perfectly recover a single clean signal realization, but rather to learn a maneuver-consistent representation that suppresses stochastic environmental disturbances while pre-serving fault-relevant spectral-temporal structures. A. Experimental Setup 

All experiments use the dataset described in Section III. The full experimental protocol is presented to allow unambiguous interpretation of all subsequent metrics and figures. 

Dataset Composition: Three quadrotor platforms are used: Type A (Holy Stone HS720), Type B (MJX Bugs 12 EIS), and Type C (ZLRC SG906 Pro2). Recordings cover six maneuver directions (forward, backward, left, right, clockwise, counterclockwise) and two health conditions (nor-mal and faulty). Fault conditions include propeller blade partial cuts and motor cap mechanical deformation. The record-ings were originally captured at 48 kHz and downsampled to 16 kHz, then segmented into non-overlapping 2-second windows ( 𝑁 = 32 ,000 samples per segment). The dataset composition, following the ICSV31 AI Challenge partition, is summarized in Table IV: a training split of 5,400 normal segments (1,800 per drone type), an evaluation split of 1,080 segments containing both normal and anomalous data span-ning six fault types (360 segments per drone type, that is, 180 segments per fault type), and a test split of 1,440 anomalous segments spanning eight fault types (480 segments per drone type, that is, 180 segments per fault type). 

Data Splitting: This work adopts the predefined dataset partition rather than constructing a custom split. The model is trained exclusively on the training split, which contains normal segments only, consistent with the unsupervised (self-supervised) setting. For threshold selection, a nominal vali-dation subset is held out from the training split; no anoma-lous data are used at any point during training or threshold selection. All reported detection metrics (AUC, precision, recall, F1-score, FPR, FNR, and the per-drone-type and per-maneuver breakdowns) are computed on the evaluation split, which contains both normal and anomalous segments and is disjoint from the training data. A sensitivity analysis over the 90th–99th percentile range confirms that detection performance is stable across this interval. The complete pipeline is therefore unsupervised in three distinct senses: no faulty segment is used for training, no faulty label is used for threshold selection, and no supervised optimization of the threshold is performed. The threshold follows solely from the statistics of the nominal validation error distribution.

D. Embedded Real-Time Implementation 

To demonstrate practical viability, the trained model was integrated into a ROS2-based onboard perception pipeline and executed on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano embedded computer. 

Hardware: NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super Developer Kit; 6-core Arm Cortex-A78AE (up to 1.7 GHz); 1024-core NVIDIA Ampere GPU with 32 third-generation Tensor Cores; 8 GB LPDDR5 (102 GB/s); NVMe SSD via M.2; JetPack 6.1 (Ubuntu 22.04 LTS, 64-bit ARM); MAXN Super power mode; ROS2 Humble Hawksbill middleware. 

Software and Numerical Precision: Python 3.10; Py-Torch 2.x for training. For deployment, the trained Stage 1 and Stage 2 models are exported to ONNX and compiled into TensorRT half-precision (FP16) inference engines that execute on the GPU Tensor Cores. No fixed-point quantization is ap-plied in the current prototype; INT8 post-training quantization is reserved for future work, which could provide a further reduction in both latency and memory footprint.

This paper presented a maneuver-conditioned acoustic fault detection framework for autonomous quadrotor drones that combines Noise2Noise-inspired denoising with conditional reconstruction learning under a strictly unsupervised paradigm. The proposed approach addresses two fundamental chal-lenges in real-flight acoustic monitoring: stochastic environ-mental noise and structured variability induced by maneuver-dependent aerodynamic effects. Unlike conventional acous-tic fault detection methods that assume stationary operating conditions or rely on extensive labeled fault datasets, the proposed framework explicitly incorporates maneuver context into both the denoising and reconstruction stages, enabling the model to distinguish between benign operational variability and genuine fault-induced deviations. The proposed archi-tecture employs a two-stage unsupervised learning strategy. First, a Noise2Noise-inspired denoising network suppresses environmental disturbances using maneuver-consistent surro-gate pairs, without requiring clean reference signals. Second, a maneuver-conditioned convolutional autoencoder models nominal acoustic behavior using only healthy-flight data, with both stages conditioning on drone type and flight direction via Conditional Batch Normalization. The anomaly threshold is selected entirely from a held-out nominal validation subset of the training split, ensuring complete unsupervised compliance. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed maneuver-aware conditioning raises the AUC from 0.561 (unconditioned baseline) to 0.884 (full model), a gain of +0.323 attributable to the combined effect of denoising and maneuver conditioning. A comparison against 11 unsupervised baselines spanning classical methods (IF, GMM, OC-SVM), reconstruction-based methods (AE, DAE, VAE, MaskedAE, MemAE), contrastive methods (AADCL), and recent state-of-the-art frameworks (SimpleNet, Dinomaly) confirms that the proposed full model attains the highest AUC and F1-score of all evaluated methods. The framework achieves an end-to-end inference latency of approximately 20 ms on an embedded NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super with a TensorRT FP16 backend, demonstrating practical viability for real-time onboard UAV health monitor-ing.
