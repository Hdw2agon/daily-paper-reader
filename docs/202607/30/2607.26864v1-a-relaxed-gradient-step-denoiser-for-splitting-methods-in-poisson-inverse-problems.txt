ABSTRACT 

Plug&Play methods combine classical variational models with learned denoisers and have achieved strong results in imaging inverse problems. Their convergence has been widely studied for Gaussian data, whereas Poisson models require additional care because of the nonquadratic Kullback–Leibler fidelity. This work introduces GSDSplit + , a splitting method built upon PnPSplit + , in which the generic denois-ing block is replaced by a relaxed Gradient Step Denoiser. The resulting method retains explicit Poisson fidelity updates. The denoiser is defined through the gradi-ent of a learned convex potential parameterized by an Input Convex Neural Net-work. Its architecture and training promote both blind denoising capability and a smoothness condition sufficient for firm nonexpansiveness. Empirical smoothness es-timates are used to select a relaxation parameter compatible with the convergence assumptions of PnPSplit + . Numerical experiments provide evidence that the trained denoiser operates in an FNE-compatible regime on the considered validation data. GSDSplit + also yields stable reconstructions outside the empirically supported range and shows substantially lower sensitivity to the ADMM parameter, while remaining effective for different blur operators and Poisson noise levels.

1. Introduction 

Image restoration is central in scientific imaging, including microscopy [20, 43], astron-omy [1, 8], and medical imaging [14, 28, 30]. The image acquisition process is described by the linear model [8, 9] 

g = N (H x ⋆ + b), (1) where x⋆ ∈ Rn is the clean data, g ∈ Rm is the recorded data, H ∈ Rm×n is the linear blur operator, b ∈ R+ is a constant background term, and N models the statistical noise. The standard assumptions on the operator H are Hi,j ≥ 0 ∀i, j, H⊤1 = 1,where 1 is the vector whose components are all equal to 1. These assumptions are commonly satisfied in practical applications, such as in microscopy and astronomy.

The noise model N depends on the physics underlying the acquisition process: the most common one is Gaussian noise, but several applications also involve speckle noise [15], salt&pepper or Cauchy [7] noise. Image restoration problems aim to recover an estimate of x⋆ from g given H, b, and the noise model. This work focuses on images corrupted by Poisson noise, which is signal-dependent and naturally arises in photon-limited acquisition systems, including fluorescence microscopy, astronomy, and emission tomography. The constraint enforces nonnegativity on the reconstructed image, which is natural since pixel values represent light intensities. The function R is a regularizer that controls the effect of noise and promotes desired image features, such as sharp edges or sparsity. The parameter β ∈ R+ is called the regularization parameter and weights the influence of R on the objective functional. The optimization problem (2) can be solved in several ways. The Alternating Di-rection Method of Multipliers [11, 24] remains popular and has led to several variants, including PIDAL [23], PIDSPLIT+ [35] and weighted TV approaches [14]. First or-der methods have been widely explored and successfully employed even in High Per-formance Computing settings [40]. Among first-order approaches, one can find also proximal gradient methods [16]. In [38], the authors observed that in an ADMM scheme the proximal step related to the regularization function R can be considered as a Gaussian denoising step: there-fore, they proposed a Plug And Play scheme (PnP), which consists of replacing the proximal step with a Gaussian denoiser D. Classical choices are the Block Matching and 3D filtering (BM3D) [18], the non-local means filter (NLM) [12] or a denoising convolutional neural network (CNN) [41]. The convergence analysis of several PnP schemes requires specific assumptions on the denoiser, such as nonexpansiveness or firm nonexpansiveness (FNE). In the FNE case, the denoiser can be interpreted as the resolvent of a maximally monotone operator. In the era of deep learning and neural networks, one possible strategy to satisfy this is to train a CNN that is FNE [32]. Other approaches include controlling the Lipschitz constant of the denoiser [21], and considering constrained variants of ADMM [6]. The investigation of PnP methods led to Regularization by Denoising (RED) theory [33], which shifted the requirement on the Jacobian of D. The RED theoretical framework is solid and well-established; nevertheless it presents some practical issues, that have been addressed and partially solved under a constrained framework (RED-PRO) [17]: RED provides an explicit denoiser-induced regularization under assumptions on the denoiser Jacobian, while RED-PRO instead adopts a constrained approach considering the fixed-point set of the denoiser. Another constrained approach (CRED) for the RED framework has been addressed in [13], using a discrepancy measure between the recorded data g and the estimated solution. Recently, Deep Equilibrium Models have 2been employed to tackle the theoretical issues of PnP methods from a different point of view [19]. PnP methods have mainly focused on image restoration problems where g is per-turbed by Gaussian noise, with the fidelity function being the least-square functional. Poisson data instead require using the KL function, as shown in (2): this functional is not quadratic and its gradient may become problematic when the estimated intensities are close to zero. In [34] a classical ADMM approach is employed: the presence of the KL functional leads to the use an iterative procedure for solving the optimization step related to such functional. The authors of [22] introduce a quantum-based denoising scheme for Poisson deconvolution, and [25] shows how the Euclidean geometry is not the most suitable one in the presence of Poisson noise, while the one induced by Breg-man divergences is more descriptive. Bayesian Poisson reconstruction has also been addressed through Langevin-based sampling methods in [27]. Primal-Dual schemes, which avoid the inversion of linear operators, are deeply studied in [31, 36]. The work [5] introduced the PnPSplit + method, which couples the PnP approach with PIDSPLIT+. It employs an ADMM scheme, according to [35]; then, the proximity operator of the regularization function is replaced by a Gaussian CNN denoiser trained to be FNE, following [32], yielding a convergence result [5, Proposition 2]. This work proposes GSDSplit + , a structured variant of PnPSplit + in which the generic PnP denoising block is replaced by a Gradient Step Denoiser [26] of the form 

Dϑ(x) = x − ∇ ψϑ(x), (4) The potential ψϑ is parametrized by an input convex neural network (ICNN) [3, 42] and trained through a residual Gaussian denoising loss, together with an empirical Hessian spectral penalty promoting L-smoothness with L close to 1. Under the ideal assumptions that ψϑ is convex and L-smooth with L ≤ 1, the operator Dϑ is FNE, and therefore fits the convergence framework of PnPSplit + . Gradient Step Denoisers form a structured class of denoisers of the form (4): their residual Id − Dϑ is a conservative vector field, because it is the gradient of a learned potential. This property connects GSDs with variational regularization, monotone operator theory and fixed-point for-mulations [26]. When ψϑ is convex and smooth, the resulting operator inherits useful nonexpansiveness properties, making GSDs particularly suitable for PnP algorithms requiring theoretical assumptions on denoising operators, such as PnPSplit + . Addi-tionally, this work considers the more general formulation that includes a relaxation parameter α:

Dϑ,α (x) = x − α∇ψϑ(x). (5) Such a choice allows to employ L-smooth network with L > 1, since the FNE condition for Dϑ,α is satisfied when α L ≤ 1. This makes it possible to employ more general ICNNs in the proposed splitting scheme. Moreover, beyond this compatibility with splitting approaches, the GSD structure makes the denoising residual an explicit gradient field, providing a natural bridge between splitting-based PnP schemes and fixed-point formulations such as RED-PRO. This connection will be investigated in future work. The main contributions of this work are the following. GSDSplit + is introduced by embedding an ICNN-based Gradient Step Denoiser into the PnPSplit + scheme for Poisson image restoration. Second, a convergence result is obtained by relating the FNE property of the denoiser to the convexity and smoothness 3of the learned ψϑ and to the relaxation parameter α. Third, a smooth ICNN architec-ture and a tailored training strategy are implemented to develop a denoiser operator satisfying the desired properties. Finally, the method is numerically assessed in terms of denoising ability, sampled FNE conditions, convergence residuals, reconstruction quality, computational cost, and sensitivity to the ADMM parameter. The experiments show that GSDSplit + attains lower peak reconstruction quality than PnPSplit + when both methods are optimally tuned, but is substantially less sensitive to an overestimate of the ADMM parameter. This work is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the novel approach, building on previous literature on PnP methods. Section 3 presents the developed denoiser, with details on its architecture and the training phase. Section 4 assesses the perfor-mance of the proposed method, its robustness with respect to its hyperparameters, and a comparison with splitting methods. Finally, Section 5 summarizes the presented approach and outlines directions for future research.

3.1. Architecture and Training 

The employed network is a modification of the ICNN [3] available in the DeepInverse 

Python package [37]. The networks encompasses 5 convolutional layers, each with 16 8filters of dimension 3 × 3 and no bias, with stride one and padding of one pixel. The initialization of the net’s weights is uniform for wz layers, while for the other layers the weights are drawn from a normal distribution. Moreover, the net is trained on square patches of 64 × 64 pixels: the numerical experience showed that this option is the best trade–off between performance and training time. The sole modification relies on the activation function: instead of using a classical LeakyReLU function, a custom smoothed version is employed. Such function reads as follows 

ρa,c (t) = at + 1 − ac log (1 + exp( ct )) , a ∈ [0 , 1] , c > 0. (8) In the numerical experiments, the parameters are set to a = 0 .1 and c = 5. The average estimate of the smoothness constant is 1 .066, with standard deviation 0 .001, and maximum value 1.07. All the estimated values are above 1 in both experiments. The experiments of Section 4 are actually performed on images and/or patches with dimension 256 × 256: a further analysis is done on patches of such dimension, and the previous results are confirmed. The average local estimate ˆL obtained on 100 samples, under the same above settings for the power iteration, is 1 .06, with a standard deviation of 0 .001 and a maximum achieved value of 1 .07. These validation experiments show that the training phase for forcing L ≤ 1 was not completely successfully. Since for the requirement for the denoiser Dϑ,α to be FNE is αL ≤ 1 and the training phase has been performed setting α = 1, this empirical evidence, on both test and out-of-sample images, shows that for the considered validation inputs such denoiser operates in an FNE-compatible regime if the relaxation parameter is chosen according to Lemma 2.3, i.e. α ˆL ≤ 1.

17 5. Conclusions 

This work introduces GSDSplit + , an extension of the PnPSplit + splitting scheme for Poisson image restoration. In the proposed method, the generic plug-and-play denoiser is replaced by a Gradient Step Denoiser constructed from a learned convex potential. This choice provides an explicit connection between the smoothness of the potential, the relaxation parameter α, and the firm nonexpansiveness of the resulting denoiser. The convergence result follows from the PnPSplit + framework whenever the denoiser is firmly nonexpansive. In particular, this property is guaranteed under the sufficient condition αL ≤ 1, where L is the smoothness constant of the learned potential. The numerical validation provides consistent empirical evidence that the trained denoiser operates in an FNE-compatible regime for α = .9 on the considered image datasets. The numerical experiments show that GSDSplit + provides reliable reconstructions for Poisson image deblurring. Although PnPSplit + attains an higher reconstruction quality when both methods use their individually selected values of the ADMM pa-rameter γ, GSDSplit + exhibits a lower sensitivity to its choice. Competitive reconstruc-tions are also obtained for some values of α outside the empirically supported FNE regime, although such cases are not covered by the convergence analysis. Nonetheless, this higher robustness to the γ parameter comes with an higher computational cost, that may be mitigated by a more tailored implementation. Future work will focus on extending the proposed splitting strategy to RED-PRO formulations for Poisson inverse problems, including constrained models and weaker as-sumptions than firm nonexpansiveness for the convergence of the resulting algorithms. A further investigation involves a possible adaptive strategy, and relative convergence analysis, for the relaxation parameter α.
