CrowdOcc: Monocular Semantic Scene Completion for Quadruped
Robots in Crowded Indoor Environments
Feiyang Chen1, Jincheng Hu1∗, Yiduo Chen1, Jihao Li2
Yue Liang1, Bingzhao Gao1, Yanjun Huang1, Yuanjian Zhang1
Abstract— Monocular semantic scene completion (SSC) for
quadruped robots remains underexplored in real crowded
indoor environments, where human-scene occlusion disrupts
static geometry and human occupancy predictions are often
incomplete or spatially misplaced. We present CrowdOcc, an
RGB-D dataset and monocular SSC framework for this setting.
CrowdOcc contains 25.1K frames from 11 indoor scenes, with
semantic occupancy annotations constructed through static-
dynamic decoupling. Our framework combines: (i) Normal-
Guided Scene Geometry Fusion (NGSGF) to complement
depth-aware lifting with surface-normal cues for occlusion-
robust geometry; and (ii) Human-Centric Sparse Interaction
(HCSI) to selectively model human-human and local human-
scene relations in 3D. Our method achieves state-of-the-art SSC
performance on CrowdOcc’s scene-disjoint test set, reaching
15.80 IoU, 11.40 mIoU, and 46.23 Human IoU, demonstrating
generalization to unseen indoor scenes.
I. INTRODUCTION
Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) predicts dense 3D
occupancy and semantics from partial observations [1]–[7].
Monocular SSC further reduces the sensing requirement to a
single RGB image, making it attractive for onboard percep-
tion on quadruped robots [8]. However, most existing SSC
datasets are built around static indoor scenes or autonomous-
driving settings, where the sensing viewpoint, scene dy-
namics, and occupancy distribution differ substantially from
those encountered by quadruped robots in crowded indoor
environments [9]–[12]. In particular, existing datasets pro-
vide limited coverage of low-viewpoint observations with
dense human presence and dynamic human occupancy. As
summarized in Table I, this leaves real crowded indoor SSC
for quadruped robots largely underexplored.
Crowded indoor scenes introduce two difficulties that
are not well addressed by conventional monocular SSC
pipelines. First, humans frequently occlude large portions
of walls, floors, doors, and other static structures. The re-
sulting incomplete observations weaken depth-based feature
lifting and make the recovery of scene geometry less reli-
able [13], [14]. Second, human occupancy remains difficult
to recover reliably in crowded scenes. Partial observations
and foreground-background ambiguity frequently lead to
incomplete human geometry and erroneous spatial place-
ment. These issues become more pronounced when the test

To address this difficulties, we introduce CrowdOcc, an
RGB-D dataset for monocular SSC in real crowded indoor
environments, together with a model tailored to scene ge-
ometry and human occupancy. CrowdOcc contains 25.1K
frames collected from 11 independent indoor scenes using
a quadruped robot. As illustrated in Fig. 1, synchronized
RGB-D, LiDAR, and IMU measurements are used for of-
fline occupancy construction, while inference takes only
a single RGB image. For scene geometry, we propose
Normal-Guided Scene Geometry Fusion (NGSGF) mod-
ule, which complements depth-based lifting with surface-
normal cues and improves the representation of partially ob-
served static structures. For human occupancy, we introduce
Human-Centric Sparse Interaction (HCSI) module, which
selects human-related voxel candidates and explicitly models
human-human and local human-scene interactions in 3D. We
evaluate cross-scene generalization on test scenes excluded
from both training and validation. Our method achieves state-
of-the-art SSC performance under this protocol.

II. RELATED WORK
A. Monocular Semantic Scene Completion
Monocular SSC recovers dense 3D occupancy and seman-
tics from a single RGB image [13], [21]. MonoScene [8] es-
tablished a representative image-to-voxel pipeline by lifting
2D features into a 3D volume and performing volumetric
context reasoning. Subsequent methods improve either the
lifting process or the resulting 3D representation [22]. Vox-
Former [23] constructs depth-guided sparse voxel queries
before dense completion, while NDC-Scene [24] performs
feature projection in normalized device coordinates to reduce
lifting ambiguity. ISO [9] further incorporates monocular
depth into indoor SSC through depth-aware feature pro-
jection and multi-scale fusion. Recent approaches explore
instance-level context, scene-adaptive decoding, Gaussian
representations, and additional geometric priors to improve
monocular occupancy prediction [14], [25]–[30]. Despite
these advances, depth remains the dominant geometric cue
for image-to-voxel lifting. Under heavy occlusion, depth
alone provides limited information about local surface ori-
entation and structural continuity. Our NGSGF complements
depth-based lifting with surface-normal cues to improve
static-scene geometry in crowded environments.
B. Human-Centric and Embodied Occupancy Perception
Vision-based occupancy prediction has been extensively
studied in autonomous-driving settings [31]–[33], with re-
cent efforts extending it to embodied platforms. Em-
bodiedOcc [16] studies online occupancy prediction for
vision-based robotic scene understanding, while Embod-
iedOcc++ [34] further improves geometric modeling for
robotic perception. OneOcc [12] considers SSC for legged
robots using panoramic vision and develops platform-specific
designs for gait-induced motion and omnidirectional sensing.
Human-aware occupancy has also been explored for model-
ing human-scene spatial relations and dynamic agents [35].
Existing instance-aware SSC methods model instance and
scene context [25], [36]. Our focus is targeted human-
centric 3D reasoning to improve the completeness and spatial
placement of human occupancy in crowded indoor scenes.
C. SSC Datasets and Occupancy Construction
Existing SSC datasets cover a wide range of sensing
platforms and environments [37]. NYUv2 [15] and Occ-
ScanNet [9], [38] focus primarily on static indoor scenes,
whereas SemanticKITTI [10], SSCBench-KITTI-360 [17],
[18], and Occ3D [11], [19] are designed for autonomous-
driving settings. WildOcc [20] extends occupancy prediction
to off-road environments. More recent datasets consider em-
bodied and panoramic perception: Human360Occ provides
synthetic human-ego panoramic occupancy, while QuadOcc
collects real panoramic data from a quadruped robot [12]. As
summarized in Table I, none of these datasets jointly provide
real crowded indoor scenes, low-viewpoint quadruped obser-
vations, and dynamic human occupancy. Dense occupancy
labels are commonly constructed by aggregating multi-frame

III. CROWDOCC DATASET
CrowdOcc comprises 25,140 RGB-D frames captured at
11 indoor scenes using a quadruped robot. Training and
validation share eight scenes, containing 16,961 and 4,265
frames, respectively. The test set contains 3,914 frames
from three additional scenes disjoint from both training and
validation, enabling cross-scene evaluation. Each frame is
paired with a semantic occupancy grid of 50 × 60 × 96 at
0.08 m resolution. LiDAR and IMU measurements are used
only for offline annotation construction, while inference takes
a single RGB image. Since static structures can be reliably
aggregated across observations whereas non-rigid humans
cannot, we construct them separately. As shown in Fig. 2,
human-free sequences are aggregated to recover the static
scene, while human occupancy is derived from synchronized
current-frame observations and subsequently refined during
voxelization.
A. Static Semantic Scene Annotation
We divide the static scene annotation pipeline into two
stages. First, we perform multi-frame static scene reconstruc-
tion from human-free sequences. Then, we transfer image-
level semantic annotations to the reconstructed 3D scene to
obtain dense semantic labels.
Multi-frame
Static
Scene
Reconstruction. We use
human-free observations to avoid introducing transient hu-

different frames are registered into a unified coordinate
system and aggregated to reconstruct a dense static scene.
Compared with single-frame observations, multi-frame ag-
gregation increases surface coverage and recovers static
structures that are partially occluded or sparsely observed
from individual viewpoints.
Semantic Static Scene Annotation. Directly annotating
dense 3D scenes is labor-intensive. We employ Grounded
SAM 2 [42], [43] to generate semantic masks from the
corresponding human-free RGB images, followed by cal-
ibrated image-to-3D projection for semantic association.
Since individual views may contain incomplete or noisy
semantic observations, projected labels from multiple frames
are integrated over the static scene and manually refined
to obtain consistent 3D semantic annotations. By construct-
ing geometry and semantics exclusively from human-free
sequences, the static branch provides a stable and dense


background representation for subsequent dynamic human
occupancy generation.
B. Dynamic Human Occupancy Construction
Dynamic humans continuously change pose and shape,
making direct multi-frame aggregation unsuitable. We there-
fore construct human occupancy from synchronized current-
frame observations and align it with the reconstructed static
scene before voxelization.
Current-frame Human Extraction and Alignment. We
first use the same vision foundation model
[42], [43] to
obtain human masks from the current RGB image. The
synchronized point cloud is projected onto the image plane,
and points within the human regions are extracted as current-
frame human observations. These observations are then
transformed into the coordinate system of the reconstructed
static scene through scene-human alignment, avoiding tem-
poral accumulation of non-rigid human geometry.
Depth-guided Human Occupancy Refinement. Human
points extracted from a single frame can be sparse and
provide only coarse spatial localization. During voxelization,
the synchronized depth map is therefore used to refine the
placement of human occupancy labels within the aligned
scene.
Human Uncertainty and Validity Masking. A human
visible in the RGB image may have insufficient 3D support in
the synchronized point cloud because of severe occlusion or
sparse point observations. We mark such regions as uncertain
and exclude them from supervision rather than assigning
unsupported occupancy or free-space labels. A separate va-
lidity mask removes geometrically invalid or ignored voxels
according to the occupancy supervision protocol. These
masks are applied after depth-guided refinement to produce
the final semantic occupancy annotations.
IV. METHODOLOGY
Our evaluation of representative monocular SSC baselines
on CrowdOcc reveals two recurring failure modes. First,
human-scene occlusion leads to discontinuous static geome-
try, particularly on wall and floor surfaces. We attribute this
to the limited surface-orientation constraints of depth-based
lifting and introduce Normal-Guided Scene Geometry Fusion
(NGSGF) module to complement depth with surface-normal
cues. Second, human occupancy is often incompletely re-
constructed or misplaced relative to the surrounding scene,
indicating ambiguity in human-scene spatial reasoning. This
motivates Human-Centric Sparse Interaction (HCSI) module,
which selects Top-K human candidates for human-human
and local human-scene interaction.
Inspired by the depth-aware lifting paradigm of ISO [9],
we formulate the overall architecture in Fig. 3 around
these two complementary designs. Given an RGB image,
a 2D UNet extracts multi-scale features F2D

C. Training Loss
Following ISO [9], we retain its scene-completion and
auxiliary depth objectives for training. The semantic oc-
cupancy annotations supervise scene completion, while the
depth maps provided by CrowdOcc are used for the auxiliary
depth objective. We denote these objectives together as Lbase.
Human occupancy labels are additionally used to supervise
the Human-Routing module. For human candidate predic-
tion, binary cross-entropy loss LBCE and Dice loss LDice are
jointly applied to supervise the predicted human probability
Ph with the human occupancy labels Yh. Invalid voxels are
excluded from the supervision. The overall training objective
is formulated as:
L = Lbase +λh [LBCE(Ph,Yh)+LDice(Ph,Yh)],
(9)
where λh is the balancing parameter for human-routing
supervision.
V. EXPERIMENTS
The acquisition platform mounts an Intel RealSense D455
RGB-D camera and a Helios-16P LiDAR on a Unitree Go1
quadruped, as shown in Fig. 1. Our model is implemented
in PyTorch with CUDA acceleration and trained on four
NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090-D GPUs. A frozen MoGe-2
ViT-L [44] provides monocular depth and surface normals.
We train for 10 epochs using AdamW [45] with a total
batch size of 4 and a weight decay of 1 × 10−4. The main
network and HCSI use a learning rate of 1 × 10−4, while
DepthNet uses 5×10−6. For the normal branch, the learning
rate is linearly warmed up from 1 × 10−5 to 3 × 10−5 in
the first epoch and then cosine-decayed to 5×10−6. We set
λh = 1.0. Unless otherwise stated, all methods follow the
same CrowdOcc split, taxonomy, and evaluation protocol.
A. Evaluation Metrics
Following standard SSC protocols, we report Precision,
Recall, and IoU for binary scene completion. All occupied
semantic classes are merged into a single occupied cate-
gory, while empty voxels form the free-space category. For
semantic completion, we report the IoU of each occupied
class and their mean IoU (mIoU) over the eight semantic
categories. Since human occupancy is central to crowded
indoor perception, Human IoU is additionally highlighted
throughout the experiments. Class frequencies in Table II
are computed over valid occupied voxels and are reported
only to characterize the semantic distribution of CrowdOcc.
Invalid and ignored voxels are excluded from both scene-
completion and semantic-completion metrics.
B. Quantitative Comparisons
Table II reports quantitative results on the CrowdOcc test
set, comprising three physical scenes disjoint from those
used for training and validation. Our method achieves 15.80
IoU and 11.40 mIoU, outperforming the strongest competing
results by +1.31 IoU and +0.68 mIoU, respectively. The
gain is particularly pronounced for human occupancy: our
method reaches 46.23 Human IoU, exceeding NDC-Scene
(44.12) and ISO (43.79) by +2.11 and +2.44 points [9], [24].
It also attains the highest recall of 37.02, compared with
34.00 for NDC-Scene and 31.32 for ISO, while maintaining
competitive precision. These results establish state-of-the-art
SSC performance on unseen scenes, demonstrating strong
cross-scene generalization.
The class-wise results show that the improvement extends
beyond human occupancy. Our method achieves the best IoU
on floor, wall, human, and door, reaching 21.12, 11.80, 46.23,
and 4.64, respectively. In particular, wall IoU improves from
9.88 with ISO to 11.80, while floor IoU increases from
20.66 with NDC-Scene to 21.12. The consistent gains on
both structural and human classes are aligned with the

C. Ablations
We conduct cumulative ablations on the CrowdOcc test
set in Table III. Starting from the ISO baseline (Q0) [9],
we progressively introduce MoGe-2 Depth [44], HCSI, and
NGSGF. In Q1, MoGe-2 replaces the original Depth Any-
thing [46] depth prior while keeping the remaining archi-
tecture unchanged, improving IoU/mIoU from 14.49/10.72
to 14.89/11.25. Adding HCSI in Q2 raises Human IoU from
42.88 to 45.77 and further improves IoU to 15.34, confirming
the benefit of targeted human-centric 3D interaction.
Finally, NGSGF yields the full model (Q3), reaching 46.23
Human IoU, 15.80 IoU, and 11.40 mIoU. Compared with Q0,
the complete model gains +2.44 Human IoU, +1.31 IoU, and
+0.68 mIoU. The cumulative improvements are consistent
with the intended roles of the three components: MoGe-
2 provides a stronger depth prior, HCSI improves human
occupancy reasoning, and NGSGF further enhances scene
geometry through normal-guided fusion.

VI. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we introduced CrowdOcc, an RGB-D dataset
for monocular SSC on quadruped robots in real crowded
indoor environments. Our method targets two characteris-
tic failures in this setting: discontinuous scene geometry
under human-scene occlusion and incomplete or spatially
misplaced human occupancy. NGSGF complements depth-
aware lifting with surface-normal cues to improve structural
geometry, while HCSI performs Top-K human-centric inter-
action to refine human occupancy and its spatial relation to
the surrounding scene. Together, these designs improve both
geometric completion and human occupancy reasoning. Ex-
periments on test scenes disjoint from training and validation
establish state-of-the-art SSC performance on CrowdOcc and
demonstrate strong cross-scene generalization.
